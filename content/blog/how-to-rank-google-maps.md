When someone nearby searches for the service you offer, the three businesses shown in Google's map box — the "map pack" — get the lion's share of the calls. Businesses below that box might as well be on page two. This guide breaks down what actually determines those three spots and gives you a concrete plan to earn one of them.

## How Google Decides Who Shows Up

Google has been fairly transparent that local rankings rest on three pillars:

- **Relevance** — how well your business matches what the searcher wants
- **Distance** — how close you are to the searcher (or the area they searched)
- **Prominence** — how well-known and well-reviewed your business is, online and off

You can't control distance — you are where you are. But relevance and prominence are entirely in your hands, and that's where ranking battles are won. Everything below targets those two pillars.

One honest caveat before we start: local rankings move over months, not days. Consistent effort beats one-time fixes, and anyone promising instant map pack placement is selling you something.

## Step 1: Fully Build Out Your Google Business Profile

Your Google Business Profile (GBP) is the single biggest factor in map pack rankings. Most businesses fill in half of it. Complete all of it:

- **Primary category:** This is critical. Choose the category that most precisely matches your core service ("Plumber," not "Contractor"). It heavily influences which searches you appear for.
- **Secondary categories:** Add every legitimate additional category that fits your services.
- **Services list:** Add each service individually with a plain-language description. This tells Google exactly what you should rank for.
- **Business description:** Write a clear paragraph covering what you do, where you do it, and what sets you apart.
- **Hours, phone, service area, attributes:** Fill in every field, and keep them accurate. If you're a service-area business without a storefront, set your service areas properly and hide your address.
- **Photos:** Upload real photos — team, vehicles, completed jobs — and keep adding them over time. Active profiles with fresh photos tend to outperform stale ones.

A complete profile isn't just an information card; it's a relevance signal machine.

## Step 2: Win the Review Game

Reviews are the most powerful prominence signal you can influence directly. Businesses in the map pack almost always have strong review profiles — not just high ratings, but momentum.

### What actually matters

- **Quantity and recency:** A steady stream of new reviews beats a burst from two years ago. Google wants evidence you're currently active and trusted.
- **Rating:** Aim for excellence, obviously, but a 4.7 with constant new reviews typically outperforms a 5.0 with eleven total.
- **Keywords in reviews:** When customers naturally mention the service and city ("fixed our AC in Brookside the same day"), it reinforces relevance. Never script this — but do ask right after the specific job is done, when details are fresh.
- **Your responses:** Replying to every review shows engagement and adds context. Keep responses professional and never include private customer details.

### The system that works

Make the ask part of your job completion routine: a text message with a direct review link, sent while the customer's satisfaction is at its peak. Text messages get far higher response rates than emails. Assign this task to a specific person so it happens every time, not when someone remembers.

Don't buy reviews, don't offer rewards for positive reviews, and don't "gate" by only asking customers you think are happy. Beyond violating Google's policies, these patterns are detectable and can get your profile penalized — the opposite of ranking.

## Step 3: Align Your Website With Your Profile

Your GBP and your website work as a team. Google cross-references them, and mismatches or weak sites hold rankings back.

- **Link your GBP to the right page.** Usually your homepage, or a location page if you have multiple locations.
- **Put your name, address, and phone number (NAP)** on your site exactly as they appear on your GBP — same formatting, same number.
- **Create a page for each core service.** "Drain cleaning" and "water heater installation" as separate, substantive pages give Google more relevance signals than one generic list.
- **If you serve multiple towns,** create genuine location pages with unique content about your work in each area — not copy-pasted templates with the city name swapped. Thin doorway pages can hurt more than help.
- **Add LocalBusiness schema markup** to your site so search engines can read your business details unambiguously.

## Step 4: Build Citations and Local Links

Prominence is partly about how often the wider web confirms your business exists and matters.

### Citations

A citation is any mention of your business name, address, and phone number — directories, Yelp, Angi, your chamber of commerce, industry associations. Two rules:

1. **Consistency:** Your NAP must be identical everywhere. "Suite 200" on one site and "Ste 200" on another, or an old phone number lingering on a directory, dilutes trust.
2. **Quality over quantity:** The major platforms plus a handful of relevant local and industry directories cover nearly all the value.

### Local links

Links from other local websites are powerful prominence signals:

- Sponsor local teams, events, and charities (most link to sponsors)
- Join your chamber of commerce and local business associations
- Partner with complementary local businesses and feature each other
- Get featured in local news — a genuine story angle helps

## Step 5: Stay Active and Behave

Google pays attention to whether your profile looks alive:

- Post updates occasionally — completed projects, seasonal reminders, offers.
- Answer questions in the Q&A section (anyone can ask; you should answer first).
- Keep adding photos.
- Update holiday hours.

And avoid the shortcuts that get profiles suspended: stuffing keywords into your business name (your name must be your real-world name), creating fake listings at virtual offices, or review schemes. Suspensions are painful and slow to resolve. Legitimate businesses win the long game.

## Step 6: Track and Adjust

You can't improve what you don't measure:

- **GBP insights:** Check monthly — calls, direction requests, website clicks, and what search terms triggered your profile.
- **Ask every caller** how they found you and record it.
- **Check your ranking from different spots.** Map results vary by where the searcher stands, so use a tool or simply search from different parts of your service area.
- **Watch the three businesses above you.** What do their profiles, reviews, and websites have that yours doesn't? That's your gap list.

Expect meaningful movement over a few months of consistent effort. Businesses that work these fundamentals steadily tend to climb; businesses that set it and forget it tend to sink.

## Quick Checklist

- [ ] Choose the most precise primary category and add all legitimate secondary categories
- [ ] Complete every field on your Google Business Profile, including services with descriptions
- [ ] Upload real photos monthly
- [ ] Build a text-message review request into your job completion routine
- [ ] Respond to every review promptly and professionally
- [ ] Match your website's name, address, and phone exactly to your GBP
- [ ] Create a substantive page for each core service
- [ ] Build genuine location pages for key towns you serve
- [ ] Audit and fix your citations on major directories for NAP consistency
- [ ] Earn 3–5 local links through sponsorships, associations, or partnerships
- [ ] Post to your profile and answer Q&A questions regularly
- [ ] Review GBP insights monthly and study the businesses ranking above you
