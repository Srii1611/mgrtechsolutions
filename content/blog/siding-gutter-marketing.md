Siding and gutter work has a marketing advantage most trades would envy: your finished jobs are visible from the street, your projects are high-ticket, and every neighborhood you work in contains hundreds of your next customers. Yet most exterior contractors still live off referrals and the occasional yard sign, feast one season and famine the next. This guide covers how to build a steady lead flow for a siding and gutter business — from owning local search to turning every job site into a marketing campaign.

## Know What You're Really Selling

Before spending on marketing, get clear on your two very different customer types, because they're reached differently:

- **Retail homeowners** replacing worn siding, adding gutters, or upgrading curb appeal. These are planned, comparison-shopped purchases — won through search visibility, reviews, and trust.
- **Storm/insurance work** after hail and wind events. High-volume, fast-moving, and won through speed, insurance process knowledge, and storm-response readiness.

Most companies do some of both. Your marketing foundation (search, reviews, website) serves both; storm response adds a layer we'll cover below.

## Own Local Search First

When a homeowner decides "we need new siding this year," their first move is Google. Being there is non-negotiable.

### Google Business Profile

Your GBP is your storefront for the Map Pack. Get it right:

- Primary category: "Siding contractor" or "Gutter service" — whichever drives more revenue — with the other as a secondary category.
- Fill every field: services (list each — vinyl siding, fiber cement, seamless gutters, gutter guards, soffit and fascia), service area, hours, and a real description.
- **Photos are your superpower in this trade.** Before-and-afters of siding transformations are some of the most persuasive images in all of home services. Upload new project photos weekly.
- Post weekly: completed projects, seasonal reminders (gutter cleaning before leaf season), and current offers.

### Reviews That Mention the Details

For a $10,000–$30,000 siding project, homeowners read reviews closely. Encourage happy customers to share specifics — the crew's cleanliness, the timeline, how the estimate matched the final invoice. Detailed reviews both rank better and sell better. Ask in person at the final walkthrough, then text the direct review link. Respond to every single one.

## Your Website: Built to Win Big-Ticket Trust

A homeowner spending five figures behaves differently than someone booking a drain cleaning. Your site needs to support a longer, more anxious decision:

- **A page per service:** vinyl siding, fiber cement (name the brands you install — people search for them), seamless gutters, gutter guards, soffit/fascia, insulation.
- **A page per town you serve** with real local projects featured.
- **A gallery that does the selling.** Organized by service and style, with before-and-after sliders if possible. This page often gets more time-on-page than any other.
- **Financing information front and center.** Big-ticket buyers filter by affordability. If you offer financing, say so on the homepage — it can be the reason someone contacts you instead of a competitor.
- **Manufacturer certifications displayed** — they signal quality and often qualify you for better warranties you can promote.
- **A real estimate process explained.** Tell people exactly what happens after they inquire: the visit, the measurement, the written quote, the timeline. Reducing uncertainty increases form fills.
- **Fast and mobile-first** — over half your visitors are on phones.

## Paid Lead Channels: What Works for Exteriors

**Google Local Services Ads.** Verified badge, top-of-page placement, pay-per-lead pricing. For gutters especially — a lower-ticket, higher-volume service — LSAs are often the fastest profitable channel.

**Google Search Ads.** Bid on high-intent terms: "siding replacement [city]," "seamless gutters near me," "James Hardie installer [city]." Tight geography, dedicated landing pages, and tracked calls. Big-ticket clicks are expensive, so conversion details matter — send ad traffic to a page about *that service*, not your homepage.

**Facebook and Instagram.** This is where exteriors shine visually. Before-and-after photo ads targeted to homeowners in specific neighborhoods — especially ones where you've just completed a job — consistently perform well for siding. Lead with the transformation, not a discount.

**Third-party lead platforms** (Angi, Thumbtack, etc.) can fill gaps in slow months, but treat them as a supplement: leads are shared with competitors, margins are thin, and you're building their brand instead of yours. Own-channel leads are almost always more profitable long-term.

## The Job Site Marketing Multiplier

Every project is a two-week advertisement parked in front of dozens of future customers. Squeeze it:

- **Yard signs on every job**, with the homeowner's permission — and ask to leave it up for a month after completion.
- **Door hangers or a friendly note** to the 20 closest neighbors: "We're replacing the siding at the Johnsons' this week — sorry for any noise, and if you've been thinking about your own exterior, here's a neighborhood discount."
- **Branded trucks and crew gear.** Your team is a moving billboard; make it look like one.
- **The five-minute neighbor habit.** When a neighbor watches the job (they always do), a friendly crew member with a business card converts curiosity into quotes surprisingly often.
- **HOA and neighborhood Facebook groups.** Ask happy customers if you can share the before-and-after in their group — authentic posts there outperform any ad.

## Storm Response: The Surge Channel

If hail and wind drive work in your region, build the machine *before* the storm:

- **Prepare storm-season assets in advance:** a dedicated landing page ("Hail damage in [City]? Free inspection"), pre-built ad campaigns you can switch on, and a door-knocking protocol that stays professional and compliant.
- **Move fast after events.** The first companies visible in a storm-hit neighborhood capture a disproportionate share of inspections.
- **Educate, don't pressure.** Content that explains the insurance claim process — "How hail damage claims actually work" — builds trust with homeowners who feel overwhelmed, and positions you as the guide rather than the storm chaser.
- **Mind your reputation.** Storm work attracts scrutiny. Knock honestly, document damage thoroughly, and never promise claim outcomes you don't control.

## Referrals and Partnerships That Feed the Pipeline

- **Realtors and property managers** constantly need exterior work before listings and between tenants.
- **Roofers, painters, and window companies** are natural two-way referral partners — you touch the same houses.
- **A referral program with teeth:** a meaningful thank-you ($100–$250 or a gift card) for every referred job that closes, announced to every past customer twice a year.
- **Past customer reactivation.** Your CRM is a goldmine: gutter guard upsells to gutter customers, siding wash-and-maintain offers, seasonal check-ins. The cheapest lead is one that already trusts you.

## Quick Checklist

- [ ] Set your GBP categories, services, and service area correctly
- [ ] Upload new before-and-after project photos weekly
- [ ] Ask for a detailed review at every final walkthrough
- [ ] Build a website page per service and per town, plus a strong gallery
- [ ] Put financing options and certifications on your homepage
- [ ] Get verified for Local Services Ads, especially for gutters
- [ ] Run Facebook before-and-after ads in neighborhoods where you're working
- [ ] Yard sign + door hangers to 20 neighbors on every job
- [ ] Prepare storm-season landing pages and campaigns before storm season
- [ ] Formalize referral partnerships with realtors and complementary trades
- [ ] Contact past customers quarterly with seasonal offers
