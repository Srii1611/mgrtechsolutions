If you run a local service business, you've probably been told you need a dozen things at once: SEO, social media, ads, email marketing, video, a blog. It's enough to make anyone close the laptop and go back to work. Here's the truth: you don't need to do everything. For most local businesses, three things deliver the large majority of the results — and they're mostly free. Do these first, in this order, and ignore the rest until they're done.

## Thing 1: Claim and Complete Your Google Business Profile

When someone in your town searches "plumber near me" or "roof repair [your town]," the first thing they see is the map with three local businesses listed under it. That box is powered by Google Business Profile, and for most local service companies, it generates more phone calls than their website, their social media, and their ads combined.

It's free. And a remarkable number of businesses have never claimed theirs — or claimed it years ago and left it half-empty.

### What to do, step by step

1. **Claim it.** Go to Google Business Profile, sign in, and either claim your existing listing or create one. Google will verify you, usually by mail, phone, or video.
2. **Fill out everything.** Every field matters: business name, categories, services, service area, hours, phone number, website, and a plain-English description of what you do. Choose the most accurate primary category you can — "HVAC contractor," not just "contractor."
3. **Add photos.** Real ones: your truck, your team, your completed work. Businesses with good photos get noticeably more calls than those without.
4. **Keep your information consistent.** Your business name, address, and phone number should be identical everywhere they appear online — website, Facebook, directories. Mismatches confuse search engines and customers.

### Why it comes first

It's free, it takes a couple of hours, and it directly controls whether you show up when nearby customers are actively looking to hire someone. No other marketing task offers that combination. Budget an afternoon this week.

## Thing 2: Build a Simple, Trustworthy Website

Once people find you — on Google, through a referral, anywhere — most of them look at your website before calling. Your site doesn't need to win design awards. It needs to answer three questions in seconds: What do you do? Do you serve my area? How do I contact you?

### The non-negotiables

- **Works on a phone.** More than half your visitors are on mobile. Pull up your site on your own phone: if you have to pinch and zoom, or the text is tiny, that's costing you jobs.
- **A phone number at the top of every page**, tappable on mobile. Don't make people hunt for it.
- **A list of your services and service area.** Be specific: "Water heater repair and installation in Springfield, Decatur, and Chatham" beats "serving the region."
- **Real photos** of your work and your team — not stock images of models in hard hats.
- **Proof you're legitimate:** reviews or testimonials, years in business, license and insurance info.
- **A contact form** that actually reaches you, with a fast response behind it.

### Keep it small and real

A five-page website done well beats a thirty-page site done poorly. A solid starter set: Home, Services (or one page per service), About, Reviews, Contact. Write like you talk — plain, direct, friendly. Homeowners hire people they feel they can trust, and overly slick copy works against that.

If your current site is old and painful, a simple rebuild on a modern website platform, or a few hundred dollars to a freelancer, is usually enough for a local service business. Don't let anyone talk you into a five-figure website before you've done Thing 1.

## Thing 3: Start Collecting Google Reviews — Systematically

Reviews are the fuel that makes the first two things work. More and better reviews improve your visibility in Google's map results, and they're the single biggest trust factor for homeowners comparing you to competitors. A business with 80 recent reviews gets the call over a business with 6 old ones almost every time.

The problem: happy customers almost never leave reviews on their own. You have to ask — every time.

### Make it a system, not a hope

1. **Get your direct review link.** Google Business Profile gives you a short link that takes customers straight to the review form. Save it in your phone.
2. **Ask at the moment of peak happiness.** Right after you've fixed the problem, the customer's relief is your opening: "Glad we got that sorted. If you were happy with the work, a Google review really helps us out."
3. **Make it effortless.** Text them the link while you're still in the driveway, or send it with the invoice. Every extra step cuts your success rate.
4. **Train your whole team.** If techs do the asking, reviews come in steadily. If it's an afterthought, they don't.
5. **Respond to every review.** Thank the happy ones. Reply calmly and professionally to the unhappy ones — future customers read your response more carefully than the complaint.
6. **Play it straight.** Never buy reviews or pay for positive ones. It violates Google's rules, can get your profile penalized, and fake reviews are usually obvious anyway.

Ten reviews a month for a year puts you ahead of most competitors in most towns. It's the highest-return habit in local marketing.

## What About Everything Else?

Ads, social media, blogging, email — these all have their place, but they multiply a foundation; they don't replace one. Ads sent to a weak website with no reviews waste money. Social media posts by a business with an unclaimed Google profile are shouting into the wind. Get the three things above working first, then add the rest one at a time as budget and energy allow.

A realistic timeline: knock out the Google profile this week, sort the website over the next month, and start the review habit today and never stop. Most businesses that do exactly this see a real difference in call volume within a few months — without spending much beyond their time.

## Quick Checklist

- [ ] Google Business Profile claimed and verified
- [ ] Every profile field completed, including categories, services, and service area
- [ ] Real photos of your work, truck, and team uploaded
- [ ] Business name, address, and phone identical everywhere online
- [ ] Website readable and easy to use on a phone
- [ ] Tappable phone number at the top of every page
- [ ] Services and service area listed specifically
- [ ] Reviews or testimonials visible on the site
- [ ] Direct Google review link saved in your phone
- [ ] Every happy customer asked for a review, every time
- [ ] Review link texted to customers right after the job
- [ ] Every review responded to — good and bad
- [ ] Ads and social media postponed until the three foundations are done
