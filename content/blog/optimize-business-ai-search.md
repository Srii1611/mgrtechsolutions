A growing number of your potential customers now type questions like "Who's a good electrician in Dayton?" into ChatGPT or Perplexity instead of Google. If you've wondered how these tools choose which businesses to name — and why yours is or isn't mentioned — this beginner-friendly explainer breaks it down in plain English, with practical takeaways you can act on without any technical background.

## First, What Are These Tools Actually Doing?

Traditional search engines show you a list of links and let you do the research. AI search engines do the research for you: they scan information from across the web, then write a direct answer — often naming specific businesses with a short explanation of why.

Two things matter about that shift:

- **Fewer businesses get mentioned.** Google shows a page of options; an AI assistant might name two or three. Being "on page one" is no longer enough — you want to be one of the named few.
- **The AI needs to *understand* your business, not just find it.** It can only recommend what it can confidently describe: what you do, where you do it, and whether others vouch for you.

The good news: these tools aren't magic, and they don't have secret information. They read the same public web your customers do. Your job is to make sure what they read about you is clear, consistent, and credible.

## What AI Search Engines Look For

Think of an AI assistant as a cautious researcher building a case for each recommendation. It weighs five kinds of evidence.

### 1. Clear, readable information about your services

AI tools learn about you primarily from text — your website, your Google Business Profile, and your listings. They're looking for plain answers to basic questions:

- What services do you offer?
- Where do you offer them?
- Who are you, and how long have you been around?
- What makes you a legitimate, trustworthy choice?

If your website says "We do it all!" but never specifically says "water heater installation in Plano, Texas," the AI has nothing solid to repeat. Businesses that describe themselves clearly and specifically get described; vague businesses get skipped.

### 2. Reviews and ratings

Reviews are the closest thing AI tools have to asking around. A business with many recent, detailed reviews on Google and other platforms gives the AI confidence that real customers vouch for you. Reviews that mention specific services ("repaired our AC the same day") are especially useful — they confirm what you do in customers' own words.

### 3. Consistency across the web

AI tools cross-check. If your website says one phone number, Yelp says another, and an old directory lists an address you left three years ago, the AI's confidence drops. Consistent name, address, phone number, and service descriptions across the web act like matching ID — mismatches raise doubts.

### 4. Mentions beyond your own website

Anyone can claim anything on their own site, so AI tools look for outside confirmation: local news mentions, chamber of commerce listings, industry directories, community sponsorships, and other websites that reference your business. Each independent mention is another witness supporting your credibility.

### 5. Recency and signs of life

A business whose last review, photo, or website update is from years ago looks like it might be closed. Fresh reviews, recent photos, and occasionally updated content tell the AI — and customers — that you're active right now.

## Why This Is Different From Old-School SEO

If you've heard about search engine optimization before, you may associate it with keywords and tricks. AI search shifts the emphasis:

| Old habit | Why it matters less now |
|---|---|
| Repeating exact keywords | AI understands natural language and intent, not keyword density |
| Thin pages built for every town name | AI values genuinely informative content, not page count |
| Chasing algorithm loopholes | AI answers synthesize broad credibility, which can't be gamed with tricks |

What matters more now is almost refreshingly human: be real, be specific, be well-reviewed, and be talked about. The businesses that have been doing honest, quality marketing all along are the ones AI tools tend to surface.

## A Simple Way to Check Where You Stand

You can audit yourself in about 15 minutes:

1. Ask ChatGPT or Perplexity: "Who are the best [your service] companies in [your town]?" Are you mentioned?
2. Ask it directly about you: "Tell me about [your business name] in [your town]." Is the answer accurate and complete, or vague and wrong?
3. Search your business name on Google. Do the top results — your site, your profiles, directories — all tell the same story?
4. Look at your review profile the way a stranger would: recent? Detailed? Reassuring?

Wherever the answers disappoint you, you've found your work list.

## Five Beginner Moves That Make the Biggest Difference

You don't need to rebuild everything at once. Start here:

1. **Spell out your services in plain language.** Every service you offer should be described clearly on your website and your Google Business Profile — what it is, who it's for, and where you do it.
2. **Add an FAQ page.** Write down the ten questions customers ask most, and answer each one directly. AI tools love question-and-answer content because it matches how people ask them questions.
3. **Get serious about reviews.** Ask every happy customer, make it easy with a direct link, and keep the flow steady.
4. **Clean up your listings.** Make your name, address, and phone identical everywhere they appear.
5. **Keep things fresh.** New photos, occasional updates, and recent reviews signal an active business.

## The Honest Big Picture

AI search is growing, but it's still one channel among many — Google, referrals, and repeat customers remain the backbone of most local businesses. The smartest way to think about AI optimization isn't as a new trick to learn, but as a reason to finally do the fundamentals well. A business that's clearly described, consistently listed, well-reviewed, and locally known is prepared for however people search — today and in whatever comes next.
