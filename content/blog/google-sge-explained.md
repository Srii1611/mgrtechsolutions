If you've searched anything on Google lately, you've probably seen it: instead of just a list of links, Google now sometimes shows a written answer at the top of the page, generated by artificial intelligence. Google calls these **AI Overviews** (you may have heard the older name, SGE — Search Generative Experience — from when it was an experiment). Understandably, business owners are asking: *Is this going to kill my website traffic? Does local search still matter? What do I need to change?* This article answers those questions honestly — no hype, no panic — with practical steps you can take now.

## What AI Overviews Actually Are

When you type a question into Google, the search engine traditionally showed you ten blue links and let you do the reading. AI Overviews change that for many searches: Google's AI reads multiple web pages, synthesizes an answer, and displays it right at the top of the results, with links to the sources it used.

A few things worth knowing:

- **It doesn't appear for every search.** Google shows AI Overviews mainly for questions and research-style searches — "how do I fix a running toilet" or "what's the best material for a patio." 
- **It pulls from real websites.** The AI doesn't invent answers from thin air; it summarizes content from pages it considers trustworthy and links to them.
- **It's still evolving.** Google continues to adjust when and how often these overviews appear, so expect the details to keep shifting.

## The Part That Matters Most: Local Searches Work Differently

Here's the reassuring news for plumbers, roofers, cleaners, and every other local service business: **searches with local intent still behave largely the way they always have.**

When someone searches "emergency plumber near me" or "roof repair [city]," they don't want an essay — they want a nearby business to call. Google knows this. For these searches, the Map Pack (those three local listings with reviews and phone numbers) still shows prominently, often at the very top, and customers still tap to call.

AI Overviews appear mostly for **informational** searches — the how-to and what-is questions — rather than "hire someone now" searches. That's an important distinction, because the searches that make your phone ring are the second kind.

So is this a non-issue for local businesses? Not quite. It changes things at the edges, and smart owners should understand where.

## How AI Overviews Will Affect Your Business

### 1. Some Informational Traffic Will Shrink

If your website gets visitors from blog posts like "how to unclog a drain," some of those searchers now get their answer from the AI Overview and never click through. This is real, and websites across many industries have seen informational traffic soften. The people who were never going to hire you — the DIY crowd — are the most likely to be satisfied by an AI answer.

The silver lining: the visitors who do click through tend to be more serious. And the searches that matter most to your revenue — "hire a plumber in [city]" — still lead to businesses, not essays.

### 2. Being a Cited Source Becomes Valuable

AI Overviews link to their sources. Websites cited in those overviews can earn clicks and, just as importantly, brand exposure: a searcher sees your company name attached to the expert answer. Being the cited authority for "how often should you service a furnace in [your state]" plants your name before the customer ever needs you.

### 3. Trust Signals Matter Even More

Google's AI is designed to prefer content from sources that demonstrate real experience and credibility. For local businesses, that's good news — you have genuine experience. But your website and profiles need to *show* it: real photos, real reviews, real expertise, a real business with a real address.

### 4. Reviews and Reputation Carry More Weight

As AI tools increasingly answer "who should I hire?" style questions, they lean heavily on the same signals Google already uses: your reviews, your ratings, your business information across the web. A business with 200 detailed, recent reviews gives AI systems far more to confidently recommend than one with eight.

## What You Should Actually Do (the Practical List)

None of this requires a new strategy. It requires doing the fundamentals properly, plus a few adjustments.

### Double Down on Your Google Business Profile

The Map Pack remains the front door for local customers. Complete every section of your profile, keep photos fresh, post weekly, and — above all — keep the reviews coming. This was your most important asset before AI Overviews, and it still is.

### Keep Building Reviews Everywhere

Steady, genuine reviews on Google (and solid presence on Yelp, Facebook, and industry platforms) are how both human customers and AI systems decide you're trustworthy. If you don't have a review request habit built into every job, build one now.

### Create Content That Answers Real Customer Questions

Yes, some informational clicks are shrinking — but content is far from dead. The key is to write what AI can't easily replicate:

- **Local specifics:** "What monsoon season does to roofs in Arizona" beats a generic roofing article. AI overviews summarize generic content; genuinely local insight stands out and earns citations.
- **Real experience:** actual jobs you've done, photos of your work, mistakes you see homeowners make, what things really cost in your area.
- **Decision-helping content:** "repair vs. replace," "what to expect during installation," "questions to ask before hiring." People still click for depth when they're close to spending money.

Write in clear, direct language. Answer the question in the first paragraph, then expand. This structure serves human readers and makes your content easy for AI systems to quote.

### Strengthen Your Trust Signals Across the Web

- Keep your business name, address, and phone number consistent everywhere.
- Maintain complete profiles on the directories that matter for your trade.
- Make sure your website clearly shows who you are: team photos, licenses, insurance, service area, and real contact information.
- Collect testimonials and case studies on your own site, not just third-party platforms.

### Don't Abandon Your Website for Social Media

AI search tools pull answers from the open web — and your website is the one place you fully control your story. A fast, clear, mobile-friendly site with service pages for everything you offer remains the hub that everything else points to.

### Keep Measuring What Matters

Watch your numbers quarterly: calls, form fills, and booked jobs by source. If website visitors dip slightly but calls hold steady, your business is fine — the lost clicks were browsers, not buyers. Judge by leads, not by raw traffic.

## What You Should NOT Do

- **Don't panic and chase gimmicks.** Anyone selling "AI Overview optimization secrets" this month is guessing. The fundamentals — authority, clarity, trust — are what these systems reward.
- **Don't stuff your site with AI-generated filler.** Publishing dozens of thin, generic AI-written articles is the opposite of what works. Search systems are increasingly good at recognizing content with no real experience behind it.
- **Don't ignore the change either.** The businesses that keep their content genuinely useful and their reputation strong will absorb this shift; the ones running on autopilot since 2018 will feel it.
- **Don't expect overnight certainty.** AI search is evolving fast. Build habits that make sense under any version of the future: great service, visible proof, steady reviews.

## The Bottom Line

Google's AI Overviews change how people research, but they don't change why people hire: a problem they can't or don't want to solve themselves, and a need to find someone trustworthy nearby. The businesses that win in this environment are the same ones that have always won online — visible in the Map Pack, loaded with recent reviews, and obviously run by real people who know their trade. AI just raises the penalty for being invisible.

## Quick Checklist

- [ ] Understand that local "hire someone" searches still show the Map Pack prominently
- [ ] Keep your Google Business Profile complete, active, and current
- [ ] Maintain a steady flow of genuine Google reviews
- [ ] Write content with local specifics and real experience AI can't replicate
- [ ] Answer the question directly in the first paragraph of every article
- [ ] Keep your name, address, and phone consistent across every platform
- [ ] Show trust signals on your site: team, licenses, real photos, service area
- [ ] Avoid thin, mass-produced AI content on your website
- [ ] Track calls and leads by source — judge by leads, not raw traffic
- [ ] Revisit your search performance quarterly as AI features evolve
