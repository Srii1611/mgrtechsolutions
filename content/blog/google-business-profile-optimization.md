Here's a strange fact about local marketing: the single most powerful asset most businesses have costs nothing, takes a few hours to set up, and then gets ignored for years. Your Google Business Profile (GBP) controls how you appear when nearby customers search for what you do — in Google Search, in Maps, and in the Map Pack that grabs the majority of clicks. Yet most profiles we see are half-filled, outdated, or completely neglected. This playbook walks you through every section of your profile, what actually moves the needle, and how to keep it working for you month after month.

## Why Your Profile Matters More Than Your Website (Sometimes)

When someone searches "plumber near me" or "roof repair [your city]," Google shows the Map Pack — three local listings — above the regular website results. Those listings come entirely from Google Business Profiles, not from websites. You could have the best site in town and still be invisible in the Map Pack if your profile is weak.

Customers make snap judgments from your profile alone: your star rating, your photos, your hours, whether you look active. Many businesses find that their GBP generates more calls than their website does — especially on mobile, where the "call" button is one tap away.

## Step 1: Claim, Verify, and Secure Your Profile

Start at google.com/business. If a profile already exists for your business (Google often creates them automatically), claim it rather than making a duplicate. Verification usually happens by phone, email, video, or postcard — video verification is increasingly common, so be ready to show your storefront, vehicle, or equipment.

Two important housekeeping items:

- **Make sure you own the profile**, not a former employee or a marketing company you no longer work with. Ownership should sit in a Google account you control.
- **Remove old duplicates.** Multiple profiles for one business split your reviews and confuse Google. Request removal or merging of any extras.

## Step 2: Get Your Core Information Exactly Right

### Business Name

Use your real-world business name — nothing more. Adding keywords ("Joe's Plumbing — Emergency Drain Repair Experts") violates Google's guidelines and can get your profile suspended. Competitors can and do report keyword-stuffed names.

### Categories (This Is a Big One)

Your **primary category** is the single strongest ranking signal in your profile. Choose the category that matches your core revenue, not everything you do. A plumber who mostly does water heater installs might still keep "Plumber" as primary and add "Water heater installation service" as a secondary.

- Review your categories twice a year — Google adds new ones regularly.
- Check what categories your top-ranking competitors use (tools or a simple search will show you).
- Add secondary categories only for services you genuinely offer and want calls for.

### Address vs. Service Area

If customers come to you, show your address. If you go to them (most contractors, cleaners, mobile services), hide your address and set a **service area** instead. List the cities and areas you actually cover — twenty nearby towns is fine; an entire state dilutes your relevance.

### Hours, Phone, and Website

- Keep hours accurate, and use **special hours** for holidays. Few things anger customers — and earn bad reviews — like driving to a "open" business that's closed.
- Use a local phone number as your primary line. If you use call tracking, put the tracking number as primary and your real local number as the additional line.
- Link your website. If you have dedicated location or booking pages, link the most useful one.

## Step 3: Fill Out the Sections Everyone Skips

This is where most profiles fall short — and where you can leap ahead of competitors.

**Business description (750 characters):** Write for customers first. What you do, what you're known for, how long you've served the area, what makes you different. Mention your city and core services naturally — but no keyword stuffing, no URLs, no promotional gimmicks.

**Services:** List every service with a short description. These help Google match your profile to specific searches ("tankless water heater install"). If you offer dozens, prioritize the most profitable.

**Products:** If you sell physical products, add them with photos and prices.

**Attributes:** These vary by industry — "veteran-owned," "women-led," "online estimates," accessibility features. Check what's available and select everything that genuinely applies. Customers filter by these.

**Opening date:** Small trust signal, worth filling in.

**Appointment/booking links:** If you take bookings online, connect the link.

## Step 4: Photos — Your Silent Sales Team

Profiles with strong photo libraries get meaningfully more calls and direction requests than sparse ones. Google's own data has long shown that businesses with photos receive more engagement, and real-world experience backs it up.

- Add at least ten quality photos at launch: your team, vehicles, completed work, before-and-afters, your workspace or storefront.
- Use real photos, not stock imagery — customers can tell, and so can engagement metrics.
- Add new photos regularly. Fresh photos signal an active business.
- Name files descriptively before uploading (water-heater-install-dallas.jpg rather than IMG_4521.jpg).

## Step 5: Reviews — The Ranking and Conversion Engine

Reviews influence both whether you rank and whether searchers choose you. The profile with 150 recent reviews at 4.8 stars beats the one with 12 stale reviews almost every time.

- Ask every happy customer, and make it easy: text or email your direct Google review link.
- Aim for steady, ongoing flow rather than occasional bursts.
- **Respond to every review** — yes, every one. Responses show prospects you pay attention, and engagement is good for your profile.
- Handle negatives calmly: acknowledge, apologize where appropriate, offer to take it offline. Never argue publicly.
- Never buy, fake, or incentivize reviews. It's against Google's terms and the risk to your profile isn't worth it.

## Step 6: Q&A and Messaging — The Forgotten Features

**Q&A:** Anyone can ask — and anyone can answer — questions on your profile. That means a competitor or a random stranger could answer incorrectly. Take control:

- Post and answer your own FAQs: "Do you offer free estimates?" "Are you licensed and insured?" "What areas do you serve?" This is completely allowed and genuinely useful.
- Monitor for new questions and answer them quickly and professionally.

**Messaging:** Customers can message you directly through your profile. Turn it on only if someone will respond promptly — slow replies here can hurt more than help.

## Step 7: Posts and Ongoing Activity

Google Posts let you publish updates, offers, and events directly on your profile. They show customers (and Google) that your business is alive. A simple rhythm — one post per week covering a recent job, a seasonal tip, or a current offer — is enough. Consistency beats volume.

## Maintenance: The 30-Minute Monthly Habit

- Check that hours, phone, and services are still accurate
- Add a few new photos
- Publish your weekly post (or batch them)
- Respond to all new reviews and questions
- Watch your **performance insights**: calls, direction requests, website clicks — trends tell you if your visibility is growing
- Remove any spam edits or fake reviews by reporting them

## Quick Checklist

- [ ] Claim and verify your profile in a Google account you own
- [ ] Remove any duplicate listings
- [ ] Use your real business name — no added keywords
- [ ] Choose the most accurate primary category plus genuine secondary categories
- [ ] Set address or service area correctly
- [ ] Keep hours current and set holiday hours
- [ ] Write a full 750-character description
- [ ] List every service with descriptions
- [ ] Select all applicable attributes
- [ ] Upload 10+ real photos, then add more monthly
- [ ] Build a review request into every completed job
- [ ] Respond to every review and question
- [ ] Seed your own Q&A with common questions and answers
- [ ] Post to your profile weekly
- [ ] Review performance insights monthly
