Every local service business owner hits the same crossroads: your website needs to be built or rebuilt, and you're staring at two very different options. One path is hiring a web agency — custom design, strategy sessions, and a price tag to match. The other is a productized website package — a fixed-price, templated service designed specifically for businesses like yours. Both can work. Both can waste your money. Here's how to tell which one fits your situation.

## What Each Option Actually Is

**A web agency** is a team — designers, developers, often strategists and copywriters — that builds a custom website around your business. Expect discovery calls, wireframes, revision rounds, and a timeline measured in weeks or months. Costs for a quality small-business site commonly run from the mid four figures well into five figures, plus ongoing maintenance fees.

**A productized website package** is a standardized service: one company, one defined offering, one price. You fill out a form with your services, service area, photos, and branding; they build your site on a proven template optimized for local service businesses. Turnaround is often days to a couple of weeks, and pricing is typically a fixed setup fee plus a monthly subscription that covers hosting, updates, and support.

Neither is inherently better. They solve different problems.

## The Real Pros and Cons

### Web Agency: Pros

- **Fully custom design.** Your site won't look like anyone else's, which matters in crowded or high-end markets.
- **Strategic depth.** Good agencies research your competitors, plan your site architecture around search terms, and write conversion-focused copy.
- **Complex needs handled.** Online booking systems, customer portals, multi-location structures, e-commerce — agencies build these.
- **You own a distinctive asset.** For established businesses where the website is a core competitive tool, custom work can pay for itself.

### Web Agency: Cons

- **Cost.** The upfront investment is significant, and change requests after launch usually bill hourly.
- **Timeline.** Custom projects regularly take 8–16 weeks — and delays are common when you're the smallest client on their roster.
- **Risk of a bad match.** Many agencies are generalists. One that's never built a site for a local service business may produce something beautiful that generates zero calls.
- **Ongoing dependency.** Custom sites often require the agency (or a developer) for updates, adding monthly costs and delays to simple changes.

### Productized Package: Pros

- **Speed.** You're live in days or weeks, not months. For a business losing leads right now, that matters more than perfection.
- **Predictable cost.** One price, known in advance, usually with hosting, security, updates, and support bundled into the monthly fee.
- **Built for your industry.** The good ones specialize in local service businesses — they already know you need tap-to-call buttons, service-area pages, review displays, and estimate forms, because they've built hundreds of them.
- **No technical homework.** You don't manage hosting, SSL certificates, plugin updates, or backups. It's handled.

### Productized Package: Cons

- **Less unique design.** Templates are customized with your branding, but the underlying structure is shared. In most local markets, customers never notice or care — but if brand differentiation is central to your strategy, it's a real limitation.
- **Feature ceilings.** Need a custom quoting calculator or a membership portal? You may hit the limits of what's included.
- **Platform lock-in questions.** Some productized services own the platform — if you cancel, you may not be able to take the site with you. This is the single most important thing to check before signing.
- **Less hand-holding on strategy.** You get a proven formula, not a custom marketing strategy. If your market is unusual, the formula may need adaptation.

## The Five Questions That Decide It

Answer these honestly and the right choice usually becomes obvious.

### 1. How fast do you need leads?

If your current site is embarrassing or nonexistent and you're losing work every week, speed wins. A productized package gets you competing this month; an agency gets you competing this quarter. A good-enough site that's live beats a perfect site that's still in a design review.

### 2. What's your real budget — including the hidden costs?

Compare total first-year costs, not sticker prices:

| Cost Item | Web Agency | Productized Package |
|---|---|---|
| Upfront build | $$$$–$$$$$ | $$ |
| Hosting/security | Often separate | Usually included |
| Updates & changes | Often hourly | Usually included |
| Your time in meetings | Significant | Minimal |
| Time to launch | 8–16 weeks | 1–3 weeks |

Also weigh the opportunity cost of your own hours. Agency projects need your input constantly — that's time not spent running jobs.

### 3. How standard are your needs?

If your wish list is "rank locally, show my work, collect reviews, and make the phone ring" — that's the exact problem productized services were built to solve. If you need custom booking logic, integrations with niche software, or multi-location architecture, you're in agency territory.

### 4. Who handles updates after launch?

This is where most small business websites die. Ask both options: "When I need to add a service or swap photos in six months, what happens — and what does it cost?" A productized package with included updates often beats an agency site that slowly goes stale because every change costs $150.

### 5. Who owns what?

Before signing with anyone, get clear answers in writing:

- Do you own the domain name? (It should be registered in your name, always.)
- If you cancel, do you get the website files and content, or does the site disappear?
- Who owns the photos, copy, and reviews displayed on it?

A slightly higher price with full ownership is almost always worth it.

## Common Mistakes Either Way

- **Paying an agency for a site a template could handle.** If you're a three-person plumbing company, a $15,000 custom site rarely produces proportionally more calls than a well-built templated one. Rankings come from content, reviews, and local signals — not custom code.
- **Buying the cheapest productized package without checking specialization.** A generic website builder service that doesn't understand local service businesses will miss the elements that actually generate calls.
- **Underestimating the content burden.** Either option needs your input: services, service areas, photos, and business details. The business owner who provides great photos and clear information gets a dramatically better result from either path.
- **Ignoring what happens after launch.** A website is a living asset. Whichever option includes ongoing updates, review integration, and support is usually the better long-term value.

## A Practical Middle Path Many Owners Miss

You don't have to choose forever. A common smart sequence:

1. **Launch on a productized package now** — get live, start ranking, start collecting reviews.
2. **Run it for 12–24 months** while your business and budget grow.
3. **Upgrade to custom only when you've outgrown the template** — and now you'll know exactly which features you actually need, because you've watched how customers use your site.

This approach limits your risk and makes any future agency investment far more informed.

## Quick Checklist

- [ ] Listed your actual must-haves (calls, bookings, galleries, service-area pages) before shopping
- [ ] Compared total first-year costs, not just setup fees
- [ ] Confirmed who handles post-launch updates and what they cost
- [ ] Verified the domain will be registered in your name
- [ ] Got written answers on who owns the site files and content if you cancel
- [ ] Checked whether the provider has experience with local service businesses specifically
- [ ] Weighed your own time commitment for meetings and feedback
- [ ] Considered starting productized and upgrading to custom later
- [ ] Asked to see live examples of sites built for businesses like yours
- [ ] Made the decision based on leads generated, not design awards
