If you've talked to more than one web developer, you've probably heard competing sermons. One swears by WordPress because "everyone uses it." Another insists you need Next.js because "it's what modern companies use." Both are right about some things and quietly wrong about others. Here's a plain-English comparison so you can make the call based on what your business actually needs — not on what your developer prefers to build.

## First: What These Things Actually Are

**WordPress** is a website platform that's been around since 2003 and powers a huge share of the web. Think of it as buying a pre-built house: the structure exists, you pick a theme (the floor plan and paint), and you add plugins (appliances) for features like contact forms, galleries, and SEO tools. You log in, click around, type your content, and publish — no code required.

**Next.js** is a modern framework for building custom websites from code. Think of it as hiring an architect and builder for a custom home: everything is built to your exact specifications, the results can be exceptional, but you need skilled people to build it and to change anything later.

Neither is "better." They're different tools that suit different situations.

## The Comparison That Matters for a Service Business

Let's compare them on the things that actually affect your leads and your sanity.

### Cost

**WordPress:** Lower upfront cost. Many contractor sites are built for a modest budget, and ongoing costs are hosting, a domain, and occasional maintenance. You can even DIY it if you're patient.

**Next.js:** Higher upfront cost — you're paying a developer to build custom. Ongoing hosting can be cheap (even free on some platforms), but any change or addition requires developer time, and developer time is the expensive part.

**Verdict:** WordPress wins for most small businesses on cost, hands down.

### Speed

**WordPress:** Can be fast, but often isn't. The average WordPress site is slowed by heavy themes, too many plugins, and cheap shared hosting. Speed is achievable but requires deliberate effort — good hosting, caching, image compression, and plugin discipline.

**Next.js:** Built for speed from the ground up. Pages are pre-generated and served nearly instantly. A well-built Next.js site almost always loads faster than a typical WordPress site.

**Verdict:** Next.js wins on raw speed potential. But a well-optimized WordPress site is fast enough to satisfy both visitors and Google — speed is a spectrum, not a pass/fail.

### Ease of Updates

**WordPress:** You log in, click "edit," type your new text or upload a photo, and hit publish. Your office manager can do it. You can add a blog post from your phone. This is WordPress's superpower.

**Next.js:** Depends entirely on how it's set up. Some developers pair it with a "headless CMS" (a separate content-editing dashboard), which can work well. Others hard-code content, meaning every text change requires a developer. Before committing to Next.js, ask exactly how you'll make routine updates. If the answer involves emailing your developer for every price change, that's a problem.

**Verdict:** WordPress wins decisively unless the Next.js setup includes a good CMS — and even then, WordPress is usually simpler for non-technical owners.

### Security and Maintenance

**WordPress:** Its popularity makes it a target. Outdated plugins and themes are the #1 way WordPress sites get hacked. Regular updates are non-negotiable, and someone has to actually do them — you, a maintenance plan, or your web company. When maintained, WordPress is reasonably secure. When ignored, it's a liability.

**Next.js:** No plugins, no database in the traditional sense, and a much smaller target for hackers. Maintenance is minimal once built. It's genuinely more secure out of the box.

**Verdict:** Next.js wins on security and low maintenance, *if* you have someone who can build and update it when needed.

### SEO Capability

**WordPress:** Excellent SEO tools via plugins (Yoast, Rank Math). You can control page titles, meta descriptions, sitemaps, and more without touching code. The structure is well understood by Google.

**Next.js:** Also excellent — arguably better technical SEO due to speed and clean code. But SEO settings depend on how the developer built it. There's no plugin dashboard; changes may require code edits.

**Verdict:** Tie, leaning WordPress for owner accessibility. Both can rank well. Neither ranks by magic — content and backlinks matter far more than the platform.

### Finding Someone to Work On It

**WordPress:** The largest talent pool in web development. If your developer disappears, you can find another WordPress person within a day. Freelancers, agencies, your nephew's college roommate — WordPress help is everywhere, at every price point.

**Next.js:** A smaller, more specialized (and more expensive) talent pool. If your developer vanishes, finding someone to take over a custom Next.js codebase is harder and pricier.

**Verdict:** WordPress wins on practical replaceability — a bigger deal than most owners realize until they need it.

## A Quick Side-by-Side

| Factor | WordPress | Next.js |
|---|---|---|
| Upfront cost | Lower | Higher |
| Ongoing dev costs | Low (DIY possible) | Higher (dev needed) |
| Speed potential | Good (with effort) | Excellent |
| Ease of self-editing | Excellent | Varies (ask!) |
| Security | Needs maintenance | Strong by default |
| SEO tools | Excellent plugins | Depends on build |
| Finding help | Easy | Harder and pricier |
| Best for | Most small service businesses | Businesses with dev budget and specific needs |

## So Which Should a Contractor Choose?

**Choose WordPress if:**

- You want to update your own content without calling a developer
- Your budget is limited
- You want the largest pool of people who can help you later
- Your site is primarily a marketing tool: pages, photos, blog, contact form

This describes the vast majority of plumbers, electricians, HVAC companies, roofers, and remodelers. WordPress, on decent hosting with proper maintenance, does everything a local service business needs.

**Consider Next.js if:**

- You have a reliable, ongoing development partner (not a one-time freelancer)
- You're building something beyond a standard marketing site — a customer portal, a quoting tool, a complex web app
- Speed and performance are worth a premium to you
- You have the budget for custom development now and for maintenance later

**Avoid Next.js if** a developer is proposing it because it's their favorite tool, you have no ongoing dev relationship, and the site is "just" your marketing site. You'll pay custom-home prices for a pre-built house's needs, and you'll be locked into their availability forever.

## The Question That Matters More Than the Platform

Here's the thing nobody selling you a website wants to say: the platform matters far less than what's on it. A fast, well-maintained WordPress site with real photos, genuine reviews, clear service pages, and helpful content will outrank and out-convert a beautiful Next.js site with thin content every single time.

Before debating WordPress vs. Next.js, make sure you have:

- Real photos of your actual work
- Specific pages for each service
- Your service area clearly stated
- Reviews and trust signals prominently displayed
- A contact form that works and a phone number that's easy to find
- Someone committed to keeping the site updated

Get those right on either platform and you'll be ahead of most of your local competition. Get them wrong on either platform and no technology will save you.

## Quick Checklist

- [ ] Defined what your website actually needs to do (marketing, not software)
- [ ] Honestly assessed your budget for building AND maintaining
- [ ] If considering Next.js, confirmed exactly how you'll make routine content updates
- [ ] If considering Next.js, confirmed an ongoing development relationship exists
- [ ] If choosing WordPress, planned for quality hosting and regular maintenance
- [ ] If choosing WordPress, committed to keeping plugins/themes updated monthly
- [ ] Prioritized content quality (photos, services, reviews) over platform features
- [ ] Verified whoever builds it will hand over full ownership and access
- [ ] Chosen based on your business needs, not a developer's preferences
- [ ] Remembered that content beats platform every time

The best website platform is the one that lets you keep great, current content in front of your customers without friction or financial pain. For most contractors, that's WordPress. For a few specific situations, it's Next.js. For everyone, it's the content that actually does the selling.
