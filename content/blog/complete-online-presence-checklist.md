When a potential customer hears about your business — from a neighbor, a truck wrap, an ad — the first thing they do is look you up online. What they find in those next thirty seconds decides whether you get the call. A complete online presence means that no matter where they look — Google, maps, reviews, social media, your website — they find an accurate, trustworthy, professional business that's easy to contact. This guide is your full setup: every asset you need, why it matters, and how to get each one right.

## The Foundation: Your Core Business Information

Before building anything, lock down your **NAP — Name, Address, Phone number** — and use it identically everywhere online. This sounds trivial but it's the single most common source of local search problems. "ABC Plumbing LLC" on Google, "ABC Plumbing" on Yelp, and "ABC Plumbing & Heating" on Facebook reads as three different businesses to search engines.

Decide on your canonical details and document them:

- Exact business name (as legally/tradename registered)
- Address in one consistent format (or service-area-only if you work from home)
- Primary phone number (ideally a tracked local number)
- Website URL, email, and hours — including holiday hours
- A 150-word business description and a 50-word short version
- A list of your core services and service areas

Keep this in a single document. You'll paste from it dozens of times, and consistency becomes automatic.

## Asset 1: Your Website

Your website is the only online property you fully own and control. Everything else is rented land. At minimum it needs:

- **Clear homepage message:** what you do, where you do it, and how to contact you — visible within five seconds on a phone.
- **A page per service.** "Services" as one page with a bulleted list doesn't rank and doesn't sell. Each service gets its own page with real detail.
- **A page per location or service area** you genuinely cover.
- **About page with real people.** Faces, story, licenses, insurance. Local customers hire people, not logos.
- **Contact options everywhere:** tap-to-call phone number in the header, a short form, and your service area stated plainly.
- **Technical basics:** HTTPS security, fast loading (under three seconds on mobile), mobile-friendly layout, and a working SSL certificate.
- **Proof:** reviews embedded or linked, photos of real work, any guarantees you stand behind.

A simple five-to-ten page site done well beats a thirty-page site done poorly.

## Asset 2: Google Business Profile

If you only do one thing from this entire article, do this. Your Google Business Profile controls how you appear in Google Search and Maps — where the majority of local customers find businesses.

Setup essentials:

- Claim and verify your profile at google.com/business.
- Choose the most accurate **primary category** and a handful of secondary ones.
- Fill every field: hours, services, service area, attributes, opening date, description.
- Add at least ten real photos — team, vehicles, completed work, your workspace.
- Turn on messaging only if someone will actually answer it.
- Post an update, offer, or photo at least monthly. Activity signals a living business.
- If you serve customers at their location rather than yours, set a service area and hide your address.

## Asset 3: Reviews Across the Right Platforms

Google reviews matter most for local rankings, but customers also check Yelp, Facebook, Angi, the BBB, and industry-specific platforms depending on your trade. A complete presence means you're covered where your customers actually look.

- **Set up profiles** on the major platforms for your industry, even if you only actively manage Google.
- **Build a repeatable review request process:** ask in person at the moment of satisfaction, then send a direct link by text or email.
- **Respond to every review.** Thank the happy ones; respond to negative ones calmly, briefly, and without arguing. Your response is written for the hundred future readers, not the one reviewer.
- **Never fake or buy reviews.** Platforms detect patterns, and removal or penalties can wipe out years of legitimate work.

A healthy target most local businesses find sustainable: a steady few new Google reviews per week, every week, forever.

## Asset 4: Citations and Directory Listings

Citations are mentions of your business name, address, and phone across the web. They reinforce your legitimacy to search engines and put you in front of people browsing directories.

Priority order:

1. **The majors:** Google, Bing Places, Apple Maps, Yelp, Facebook, Better Business Bureau.
2. **Local:** your chamber of commerce, city/regional business directories, local news business listings.
3. **Industry:** Angi, HomeAdvisor, Thumbtack, Houzz (home services); Avvo (legal); Healthgrades (medical); TripAdvisor (hospitality) — whatever applies to your trade.
4. **Data aggregators and GPS:** ensure your info flows to navigation apps so customers can literally drive to you.

Audit annually: search your business name and phone number, claim any unclaimed listings, and fix outdated addresses or old phone numbers.

## Asset 5: Social Media (the Right Amount)

You don't need to be on every platform. You need to look alive on the one or two where your customers spend time.

- **Facebook** is non-negotiable for most local service businesses: it's where recommendations happen in community groups, and an empty page with a 2019 cover photo looks abandoned. Post a few times a month — completed jobs, team news, seasonal tips.
- **Instagram** suits visual trades (landscaping, remodeling, cleaning). Before-and-afters perform well.
- **Nextdoor** is powerful for home services — neighbors actively ask for recommendations there.
- **LinkedIn** matters mainly if you chase commercial contracts.
- Complete every profile fully: consistent NAP, link to your website, current photos, and accurate hours.

The rule: a dormant profile is worse than no profile. Choose only what you can maintain.

## Asset 6: Basic Paid Visibility (Optional but Powerful)

Once the foundations are set, two paid options punch above their weight for local businesses:

- **Google Local Services Ads:** appear above everything else with a trust badge; pay per lead, not per click. Verification takes time — start the process early.
- **Google Search Ads:** capture high-intent searches ("emergency electrician near me") while your organic rankings mature. Start with a tight radius and your most profitable services.

Even a modest budget works if your website converts and you track cost per lead. Skip paid entirely until your GBP, reviews, and website are in order — ads amplify what's already there, for better or worse.

## Asset 7: Measurement and Maintenance

A presence you don't measure slowly rots. Set up:

- **Google Analytics and Search Console** on your website (free) to see traffic and search queries.
- **Call tracking** so you know which channel produces calls.
- **A simple monthly scorecard:** website visitors, calls, form fills, new reviews, map ranking for your top three services.
- **A quarterly audit:** click every phone link, test every form, check hours across platforms, update seasonal photos, fix any listings that drifted.

Block one hour per month for this. Businesses that measure improve; businesses that don't just hope.

## Putting It in Order

If you're starting from scratch, work this sequence:

1. **Week 1:** Lock your NAP document. Claim Google Business Profile. Check your website's mobile basics.
2. **Week 2:** Complete Facebook and the major directories. Fix any citation inconsistencies.
3. **Week 3:** Build your review request process and use it on every job going forward.
4. **Week 4:** Set up analytics, call tracking, and your monthly scorecard.
5. **Ongoing:** Reviews weekly, GBP posts monthly, full audit quarterly.

A complete online presence isn't a project with an end date — it's a light ongoing habit. But the heavy lifting happens once, and everything after that is maintenance.

- [ ] Document your exact business name, address, phone, hours, and descriptions in one place
- [ ] Website: clear homepage, page per service, page per area, tap-to-call, fast on mobile
- [ ] Claim, verify, and fully complete your Google Business Profile
- [ ] Add 10+ real photos to your GBP and post monthly
- [ ] Set up profiles on Yelp, Facebook, BBB, and your industry's key directories
- [ ] Fix all inconsistent or outdated listings
- [ ] Build a review request into every completed job
- [ ] Respond to every review, positive and negative
- [ ] Keep one or two social profiles active — not five dormant ones
- [ ] Install Google Analytics, Search Console, and call tracking
- [ ] Create a monthly scorecard: visitors, calls, leads, reviews, rankings
- [ ] Schedule a quarterly full audit of every link, form, and listing
