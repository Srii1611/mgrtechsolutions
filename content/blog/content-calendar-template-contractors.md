You already know you should be posting content — blogs, photos, seasonal tips — but "post more" isn't a plan, and random bursts of activity followed by three silent months doesn't move the needle. What you need is a calendar: a simple, repeatable schedule that matches your trade's busy seasons and your real available time. Below is a complete 12-month template you can copy, plus the system to keep it running without hiring a marketing team.

## Why a Calendar Beats Inspiration

Contractors who rely on "posting when I think of it" almost always stop posting. A calendar fixes that because it:

- **Removes the daily decision.** You're never staring at a blank screen wondering what to write — the topic was decided months ago.
- **Matches demand.** You publish furnace content in early fall, not January. Seasonal timing means your content shows up right when people start searching.
- **Compounds over time.** Twelve months of consistent posts gives you dozens of pages that can rank in Google, photos for ads, and proof for nervous customers.

One honest warning: content marketing is a slow build. Many contractors find it takes four to six months of steady publishing before traffic and calls noticeably increase. The calendar keeps you going through that quiet stretch.

## The Basic Structure: Three Layers

Your calendar has three repeating layers. Layer them together and you're posting weekly without ever scrambling.

| Layer | Frequency | Examples | Time Required |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Anchor content** (blog posts, guides) | 2 per month | "How much does a roof replacement cost in [Town]?" | 2–3 hrs each (or outsourced) |
| **Proof content** (photos, reviews, project spotlights) | 1–2 per week | Finished job photos, 5-star review graphics, before/afters | 10 min each |
| **Engagement content** (tips, seasonal reminders) | 1–2 per week | "3 signs your water heater is failing," holiday hours, crew intros | 10 min each |

That's roughly 8 anchor posts a month spread across blog, Google Business Profile, Facebook, and Instagram — achievable for almost any business.

## The 12-Month Template

Adjust the specifics to your trade, but keep the rhythm: plan next month during the last week of the current one.

### January — New Year, New Projects
- **Anchor:** "Home Projects Worth Planning (and Budgeting) This Year"; "Why Winter Quotes Can Be a Smart Move"
- **Proof:** Best-of-last-year project roundup
- **Engagement:** New Year greeting, winter maintenance tip, "book spring work early" reminder

### February — Pre-Season Push
- **Anchor:** "How to Choose a Contractor Without Getting Burned"; "Questions to Ask Before Signing Any Estimate"
- **Proof:** Review spotlights (people are researching now for spring)
- **Engagement:** Spring booking countdown, crew introduction post

### March — Spring Kickoff
- **Anchor:** "Spring Home Maintenance Checklist for [Your Region]"; a cost guide for your #1 spring service
- **Proof:** First spring jobs photographed
- **Engagement:** Storm-season prep tips, before/after from winter work

### April — Peak Research Season
- **Anchor:** "Repair vs. Replace: How to Decide" (works for nearly every trade); a materials comparison post
- **Proof:** Weekly project spotlights begin
- **Engagement:** Tax-refund season nudge ("invest it in your home"), spring storm tips

### May — Busy Season Begins
- **Anchor:** "How Long Does [Your Main Project] Really Take?"; financing/options explainer
- **Proof:** Two project spotlights per week if you can — you're doing the work anyway
- **Engagement:** Memorial Day post, scheduling-ahead reminder

### June — Full Swing
- **Anchor:** A problem-diagnosis post ("Why Your [System/Surface] Is Failing"); neighborhood-focused post for a town you want more work in
- **Proof:** Time-lapse or walk-through video of a signature job
- **Engagement:** Summer prep tips, Father's Day post, heat-related maintenance advice

### July — Stay Visible While Busy
- **Anchor:** "What Makes a Quote Higher or Lower? (Honest Breakdown)"; seasonal service guide
- **Proof:** Keep photographing — this content fuels your fall and winter posts
- **Engagement:** Holiday hours, quick DIY-vs-call-a-pro tips

### August — Pre-Fall Positioning
- **Anchor:** "Fall Projects to Book Before the Rush"; "How [Your Service] Holds Up Through Winter"
- **Proof:** Mid-summer project gallery update
- **Engagement:** Back-to-school timing angle ("get it done while the kids are in school"), end-of-summer checklist

### September — Fall Season Opens
- **Anchor:** Fall maintenance checklist; "Why Fall Is Ideal for [Your Service]"
- **Proof:** Storm-damage or weather-related repair photos (high demand content)
- **Engagement:** Winterization reminders begin, booking-urgency post

### October — Heating/Winter Prep Searches Spike
- **Anchor:** "Preparing Your Home for Winter: What to Check First"; a cost guide for your top fall service
- **Proof:** Before/afters from fall jobs
- **Engagement:** Halloween-adjacent fun post, first-freeze warnings

### November — Gratitude and Urgency
- **Anchor:** "Home Projects to Finish Before the Holidays"; year-end planning guide
- **Proof:** Customer thank-you posts, review roundups
- **Engagement:** Thanksgiving message, winter emergency-service reminder

### December — Quietly Plant Next Year's Seeds
- **Anchor:** "Year in Review: Our Favorite Projects of [Year]"; "Home Improvement Resolutions for Next Year"
- **Proof:** Year-end highlight reel or slideshow
- **Engagement:** Holiday hours, thank-you message, gift-card or referral nudge

## How to Actually Execute This in 90 Minutes a Week

A template is useless without a routine. Here's a realistic one:

1. **Last Friday of each month (60 min):** Pick next month's topics from the template. Assign dates. Draft the two anchor posts — or send the topics to a writer.
2. **During every job (0 extra min):** Snap 5–10 photos. This is your proof content pipeline. One folder per job, named by street or customer.
3. **Monday morning (20 min):** Schedule the week's posts. Free tools (Meta Business Suite, Google's own posting) let you queue a week of content in one sitting.
4. **After each completed job (2 min):** Send the review link. Reviews are content too — screenshot the best ones monthly and turn them into graphics.

### Batch Everything

Trying to create content daily is how calendars die. Instead:

- Write both monthly anchor posts in one sitting, or record yourself answering the question out loud on a drive and have it transcribed.
- Photograph every job in batches — you already do the work; the photos are free.
- Create simple graphics (review screenshots, before/after side-by-sides) once a month with a free tool like Canva.

## Repurpose Like a Pro

One piece of anchor content should become at least five posts:

1. The blog post itself on your website
2. A Google Business Profile update linking to it
3. A Facebook post summarizing the key point
4. A short video of you reading the main tip on camera
5. A tip graphic pulled from the post's checklist

Suddenly two blog posts a month becomes 10+ pieces of content — with almost no extra thinking.

## Measuring Whether It's Working

Check these once a month, no more:

- **Google Business Profile insights:** Are calls and direction requests trending up quarter over quarter?
- **Website traffic:** Are your anchor posts starting to appear in search? (Google Search Console shows this free.)
- **Lead source:** Ask every caller "how did you hear about us?" and tally the answers in a notebook.

Judge by quarters, not weeks. If month three looks quiet but month six shows a climb, the calendar is doing its job.

## Quick Checklist

- [ ] Print or save the 12-month template and swap in your trade's peak seasons
- [ ] Block 60 minutes on the last Friday of each month for planning
- [ ] Set up a photo folder system on your phone for job documentation
- [ ] Choose your channels (website + Google Business Profile + one social platform is plenty)
- [ ] Schedule posts weekly in one 20-minute batch
- [ ] Send a review request after every completed job
- [ ] Plan each anchor post to be repurposed into at least four other pieces
- [ ] Review calls, traffic, and lead sources monthly — judge results quarterly
- [ ] Commit to six full months before evaluating whether it's working
