You don't need to become an SEO expert to win local search — but you do need to stop making the mistakes that keep your competitors above you in the map results. Most contractors aren't losing leads because of some advanced ranking factor. They're losing them over basics: neglected profiles, inconsistent information, and websites that answer nothing. Here are the nine most common mistakes, why each one costs you jobs, and exactly how to fix it.

## Mistake 1: Treating Your Google Business Profile as "Set and Forget"

The single biggest lead channel for most contractors is the Google map pack — and the most common mistake is claiming the profile once and never touching it again. Google favors profiles that show signs of an active, real business.

**The fix:** Spend 15 minutes a week on it:

- Add a photo from a recent job (profiles with fresh photos consistently perform better)
- Post a short update — a finished project, a seasonal tip, a holiday-hours note
- Check that hours, services, and service area are still accurate
- Answer any questions posted in the Q&A section before random strangers do

## Mistake 2: Inconsistent Business Name, Address, and Phone Number

This one kills rankings silently. If your website says "Smith's Plumbing LLC," Google says "Smiths Plumbing," and Yelp shows an old phone number from two years ago, search engines lose confidence that you're one legitimate business — and rankings drop.

**The fix:** Pick one exact version of your business name, address, and phone number (often called NAP). Then audit every place you're listed — Google, website, Facebook, Yelp, Angi, Nextdoor, Bing, chamber of commerce — and make them identical, character for character. Do this audit once a year, and any time you move or change numbers.

## Mistake 3: Choosing the Wrong (or Too Few) Business Categories

Your Google Business Profile category is one of the strongest ranking signals you control. Many contractors pick one generic category and stop — or worse, pick the wrong primary one. A company that does 80% HVAC but lists "Handyman" as its primary category will struggle to rank for AC repair.

**The fix:** Choose the primary category that matches your most valuable service ("HVAC contractor," "Plumber," "Roofing contractor"), then add every relevant secondary category. Review them yearly — Google adds new categories regularly, and your service mix changes.

## Mistake 4: A Website with No Service or Location Pages

If your entire website is a homepage and a contact page, you've given Google almost nothing to rank. Each service you offer and each town you serve is a separate search opportunity — and you can't rank for "water heater repair in [Town]" if no page on your site talks about it.

**The fix:** Build:

- One page per core service (not a bullet list — a real page with 400+ words, photos, and a call to action)
- One page per priority town, written with genuine local detail, not copy-paste with the town name swapped
- An FAQ or blog answering the questions customers ask on every estimate call

## Mistake 5: Ignoring Reviews — or Worse, Faking Them

Reviews influence both rankings and the homeowner's decision to call. The common failures: never asking, letting reviews go stale (a profile whose last review is from two years ago looks closed), and leaving negative reviews unanswered. And a warning: buying fake reviews or offering incentives for positive ones violates Google's policies and can get your profile suspended — a catastrophe for lead flow.

**The fix:**

- Text every happy customer a direct review link the day the job wraps
- Reply to every review within a few days — professionally, especially the bad ones
- Aim for steady growth (a few reviews a week), not a burst followed by silence

## Mistake 6: A Slow, Broken Mobile Experience

Most homeowners search for contractors on a phone — often standing in front of the broken thing. If your site takes ten seconds to load, has tiny text, or a phone number that isn't tappable, they hit the back button and call the next contractor. Google notices those bounces, too.

**The fix:**

- Test your site on your own phone: Can you call yourself in one tap? Can you read it without zooming?
- Compress photos before uploading (huge job photos are the #1 cause of slow contractor sites)
- Run Google's free PageSpeed Insights test and hand the report to whoever manages your site

## Mistake 7: Writing Content for Google Instead of Customers

Some contractors hear "you need content" and publish thin, keyword-stuffed pages that say nothing. Google has spent two decades learning to ignore exactly that — and AI search tools are even better at skipping fluff.

**The fix:** Write only content that answers real questions you hear on real jobs:

- "How much does [service] cost in [Region]?" — with honest ranges
- "Repair vs. replace: how to decide"
- "What to check before you call a [trade]"
- Seasonal prep guides for your climate

One genuinely helpful article a month outperforms ten pages of filler.

## Mistake 8: Never Tracking Where Leads Actually Come From

Without tracking, you can't tell whether your SEO efforts work — or whether that directory you're paying $300 a month for has produced a single call. Many contractors are surprised to learn where their leads actually originate once they start counting.

**The fix (all free or cheap):**

- Ask every caller "How did you hear about us?" and write it down — every single time
- Check Google Business Profile insights monthly (calls, direction requests, website clicks)
- Set up Google Search Console to see which searches bring people to your site
- Consider a call-tracking number for any paid channel

## Mistake 9: Giving Up After Six Weeks

The most expensive mistake of all. Local SEO compounds: reviews accumulate, pages age and climb, Google gains confidence in your business. Contractors who quit at week six pay all the costs and collect none of the returns. Many contractors find the noticeable lift comes around months four through six, then keeps building.

**The fix:** Commit to a six-month minimum. Judge progress quarterly against your starting point, not weekly against your hopes.

## The Pattern Behind All Nine

Notice what these mistakes have in common: none of them are technical wizardry. They're basics — accurate information, fresh proof of work, answered questions, steady reviews — done consistently. That's genuinely good news. It means the gap between you and the contractor outranking you is usually effort and consistency, not budget or expertise. And that's a gap you can close.

## Quick Checklist

- [ ] Google Business Profile updated weekly with photos and posts
- [ ] Business name, address, and phone number identical everywhere online
- [ ] Primary and secondary Google categories match your most valuable services
- [ ] One website page per core service and per priority town
- [ ] Review link texted to every customer at job completion
- [ ] Every review answered — positive and negative
- [ ] Website loads fast on mobile with tap-to-call number visible
- [ ] Content answers real customer questions instead of stuffing keywords
- [ ] Every lead asked "How did you hear about us?" and the answers tallied
- [ ] Six-month commitment made before evaluating results
