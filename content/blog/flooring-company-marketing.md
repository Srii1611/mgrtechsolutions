Flooring is a visual, high-ticket, once-a-decade purchase. Homeowners spend weeks comparing materials, scrolling photos, and worrying about whether the installer will do a clean job — which means your marketing needs to show beautiful work, answer cost questions honestly, and make you the obvious safe choice. Here's how flooring companies can turn that long decision process into a steady pipeline of booked projects.

## Know What Makes Flooring Buyers Different

Unlike an emergency plumber, your customer isn't in a rush — they're in research mode. The typical journey looks like:

1. Inspiration: browsing photos of hardwood, LVP, and tile on social media and search
2. Comparison: "LVP vs hardwood," "cost to refinish vs replace"
3. Shortlisting: checking reviews, galleries, and before-and-afters
4. Contact: requesting an in-home measurement or showroom visit

Your marketing wins when it shows up at all four stages — especially the visual ones. If your work isn't photographed and published, for most buyers it doesn't exist.

## Build a Photo Engine, Not Just a Camera Roll

Your finished floors are your best salespeople, but only if people can see them. Make photography part of every job:

- **Shoot before, during, and after.** The ripped-out carpet next to the new oak is a more persuasive story than a pretty room alone.
- **Get the details.** Close-ups of transitions, stair noses, herringbone patterns, and flush vents signal craftsmanship better than wide shots.
- **Capture a quick video walkthrough** on your phone — 30 seconds of someone walking across the finished floor performs well everywhere.
- **Ask permission** to photograph and to mention the neighborhood ("white oak install in a 1920s craftsman in [area]"). Local context builds local trust.
- **Organize everything** in folders by material and room type so you can reuse it on your website, ads, and social posts for years.

One habit that pays for itself: the installer who finishes the job takes five photos before packing up. No exceptions.

## Your Website: Showroom First, Brochure Second

Flooring websites win or lose on the gallery. Structure yours around what buyers want to see:

- **A filterable project gallery** — by material (hardwood, LVP, tile, carpet), by room (kitchen, basement, stairs), and ideally by color tone. Let people find floors that look like the home they have.
- **One page per service:** hardwood installation, refinishing, LVP/laminate, tile, carpet, and repairs. Each with photos of *your* work, the brands you carry, and a rough idea of pricing or what affects cost.
- **A clear process section.** Buyers fear chaos: dust, delays, furniture moving, surprise charges. Walk them through measurement, tear-out, installation day, and cleanup. Certainty sells.
- **Easy next steps everywhere:** "Schedule a free in-home measurement" or "Visit our showroom" buttons on every page, plus tap-to-call on mobile.
- **Reviews embedded near the gallery** — praise for clean crews and on-time finishes right beside the photos of the work.

## Answer the Money Questions Before They Ask

Cost is the biggest source of anxiety in flooring, and almost nobody in the industry talks about it openly. That's your opening. Publish honest content like:

- "What does it cost to install LVP in a 1,200 sq ft home?" — give ranges and the factors that move the price (subfloor condition, furniture moving, old floor removal, material grade).
- "Refinish or replace? How to decide for your hardwood floors."
- "The hidden costs in cheap flooring quotes" — explain what low bids often leave out.

Companies that answer pricing questions openly tend to attract better-qualified leads: people who call already understanding the ballpark, ready to talk about their project instead of sticker-shocked.

## Local SEO: Get Found Where Projects Start

When research turns into "flooring company near me," you need to be visible.

- **Google Business Profile:** Category "Flooring contractor," every service listed, and a steady stream of new project photos. Post weekly — a fresh install photo with a one-line caption takes two minutes.
- **Reviews:** Ask at the walkthrough, when the customer is standing on their new floor and happiest. Respond to every one, naming the material ("glad the hickory in your kitchen turned out the way you pictured").
- **Location pages:** If you serve several towns, build a page for each with local photos and real details, not a find-and-replace template.
- **Directories:** Houzz, Angi, Yelp, and your local home builder or remodeler associations. Keep name, address, and phone identical everywhere.

## Social Media: Where Flooring Naturally Shines

Few trades are as suited to visual platforms as yours.

- **Before-and-after posts** are your bread and butter — same angle, same room, dramatic difference.
- **Short videos:** time-lapses of an install, the satisfying moment a final board clicks in, a crew member explaining why acclimation matters.
- **Educational snippets:** "Why we don't install hardwood in basements," "What those gaps between boards in winter actually mean." Expertise builds the trust that closes $8,000 projects.
- **Tag the neighborhood** (with permission) and use local hashtags — your next customer is usually three streets away from your last one.

Consistency beats production value. Two genuine phone-shot posts a week outperform one polished post a month.

## Partnerships That Feed You Projects

Some of the best flooring leads never hit Google at all:

- **Realtors and property managers** — homes need floors before listing and between tenants.
- **Interior designers and remodelers** — they pick materials; you install them.
- **General contractors and painters** — trades that are in the home right before flooring goes in.
- **Insurance restoration contacts** — water damage jobs often include full floor replacement.

Buy coffee, share referrals both ways, and make it easy for partners to hand you work: a simple one-page overview of your services and service area they can forward to clients.

## Stay in Touch With Past Customers

Floors last years, but customers move, renovate other rooms, and have friends who need floors now.

- Collect email addresses on every job and send a quarterly note: seasonal care tips ("winter humidity and your hardwood"), a recent project photo, a referral thank-you offer.
- A year after installation, send a check-in: "How's the floor holding up? Here's how to keep the finish looking new." It's service, not sales — and it keeps your name attached to the floor under their feet when their neighbor asks who did it.

## Quick Checklist

- [ ] Before/during/after photos taken on every job
- [ ] Filterable website gallery by material and room
- [ ] One page per service with real photos and pricing guidance
- [ ] Installation process explained step by step on the site
- [ ] Honest cost content published and kept current
- [ ] Google Business Profile updated weekly with project photos
- [ ] Review requested at every final walkthrough
- [ ] Two or more social posts per week (before-and-afters, videos, tips)
- [ ] Referral relationships with realtors, designers, and other trades
- [ ] Quarterly email to past customers with care tips and project photos
