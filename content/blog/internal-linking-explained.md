If you've ever read anything about SEO, you've probably heard that links matter. What usually gets left out is that the easiest links to get are the ones you create yourself — between the pages of your own website. That's internal linking, and for a local service business it's one of the simplest, most overlooked ways to help your site show up in search. No technical skills required. Here's what it is, why it works, and how to do it this week.

## What Internal Linking Actually Means

An internal link is simply a link from one page of your website to another page of your website. When your homepage mentions "water heater repair" and those words link to your water heater repair page, that's an internal link. Your navigation menu is internal links. So is the "See our drain cleaning services" sentence at the bottom of a blog post.

Two groups of "readers" use these links:

- **People**, who click them to move around your site and find what they need.
- **Search engines**, which follow them to discover your pages and understand what your site is about.

Most business owners build their site, set the menu, and never think about links again. That's a missed opportunity, because a few deliberate linking habits can meaningfully improve how your pages perform in search.

## Why Internal Links Matter for Local SEO

### They help search engines find and understand your pages

Search engines explore the web by following links. A page that nothing links to may never get noticed or ranked at all. A page linked from your homepage and several other pages looks important — because your own site is treating it as important. Every service page, every location page, every blog post should be reachable through links from elsewhere on your site, not just buried in a menu.

### They tell Google what each page is about

The clickable text of a link — called anchor text — is a strong hint about the topic of the page it points to. A link that says "water heater repair in Springfield" tells a search engine far more than a link that says "click here." Multiply that across your whole site and you've painted a clear picture of what you do and where you do it.

### They spread value around your site

Pages earn credibility in search partly through links from other websites — and for most local businesses, the homepage collects most of those. Internal links pass some of that homepage strength along to your service pages and blog posts. Think of it as water pressure: internal links are the pipes that carry it to the rooms that need it.

### They keep visitors on your site longer

A homeowner reading your article about furnace noises who sees a link to "our furnace repair services" often clicks it. Visitors who view multiple pages are more likely to call, and engagement signals don't hurt your rankings either.

## The Pages That Matter Most for a Local Service Business

Before linking, know your priorities. For a typical contractor site, the money pages are:

- **Service pages** — "Water Heater Repair," "Drain Cleaning," "AC Installation"
- **Location pages** — "Plumbing Services in Springfield" (if you serve multiple towns)
- **Contact or quote page**

Your internal linking should consistently funnel attention toward these pages. Blog posts and informational content exist largely to attract visitors and then route them, via internal links, to the pages that turn them into customers.

## How to Do It Right: Practical Rules

### 1. Link from every blog post to a relevant service page

Every article you publish should include at least one link to the service it relates to. Writing about "signs your water heater is failing"? Link the phrase to your water heater repair page in the first few paragraphs and again near the end. This is the single highest-value internal linking habit.

### 2. Use descriptive anchor text

Link the words that describe the destination — "our drain cleaning services" — not "click here" or "learn more." Keep it natural: the sentence should read normally to a human.

### 3. Link service pages to each other where it makes sense

Your furnace repair page can naturally mention and link to your furnace maintenance page. Your roofing page might link to gutter installation. These sideways links help visitors and spread ranking value among your money pages.

### 4. Connect service and location pages

If you have pages for each town you serve, link them to the relevant services: "We provide septic installation in Chatham and Rochester," with each town linked. This reinforces the connection between what you do and where you do it — the core of local SEO.

### 5. Don't overdo it

A page stuffed with thirty internal links looks spammy and dilutes the value of each one. A handful of relevant, well-placed links per page is plenty. If a link wouldn't help a real reader, skip it.

### 6. Make sure important pages aren't orphaned

Check that every page you care about is linked from somewhere obvious — the homepage, a services overview page, or relevant blog posts. A page only reachable through the menu is one tap away from being forgotten by search engines and visitors alike.

## A 30-Minute Internal Linking Tune-Up

Here's a quick audit you can do yourself:

1. **List your five most important pages** — usually your top services.
2. **Open your homepage.** Does it link directly to each of those five with descriptive text? If not, add links.
3. **Open your five most-visited blog posts or pages** (guess if you don't have analytics). Does each link to the relevant service page? Add links where they're missing.
4. **Check for dead ends.** Open each service page: does it link onward — to contact, to related services, to a location page? No page should be a cul-de-sac.
5. **Fix your anchor text.** Search your site for "click here" and replace those links with descriptive phrases.

Do this once, then make "link to a service page" a standard step whenever you publish anything new.

## What Not to Expect

Internal linking is a multiplier, not a miracle. It works on top of the fundamentals — a decent website, a complete Google Business Profile, reviews, and genuinely useful content. On its own it won't rescue a weak site, and results take weeks or months to show, not days. But it's free, it's fully within your control, and unlike most SEO advice, you can finish it in an afternoon and never pay anyone a dime.

## Quick Checklist

- [ ] Five most important service or location pages identified
- [ ] Homepage links directly to each important page
- [ ] Every blog post links to at least one relevant service page
- [ ] Anchor text describes the destination (no "click here")
- [ ] Service pages link to related services and location pages
- [ ] Location pages linked from service pages and blog posts
- [ ] Every important page reachable through in-content links, not just the menu
- [ ] No page is a dead end — each links onward to contact or related pages
- [ ] Link counts kept modest and relevant on every page
- [ ] "Add internal links" made a standard step in publishing new content
