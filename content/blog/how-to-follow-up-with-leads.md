Here's an uncomfortable truth about service businesses: most leads aren't lost to competitors' lower prices. They're lost to silence. The homeowner who filled out your form Tuesday night heard nothing until Thursday — so they hired the company that called back in twenty minutes. A disciplined follow-up sequence is the cheapest revenue you'll ever generate. Here's how to build one that actually books jobs.

## Why Follow-Up Is Where the Money Is

Consider what happens to a typical lead in a typical shop:

- It lands in an email inbox nobody watches
- Someone calls once, gets voicemail, and moves on
- The estimate goes out and is never mentioned again

Meanwhile, the customer's intent is highest in the first hours after they reach out, and it decays fast. Many businesses find that responding within minutes — not hours — dramatically changes how many inquiries turn into booked jobs, simply because you're the first live conversation.

The other leak: estimates. A large share of quotes in this industry are never followed up even once. The customer got three bids, got busy, and forgot. The company that politely reappears at the right moment often wins by default.

## The Speed-to-Lead Rule

Before sequences and scripts, fix response time. It's the single highest-leverage change you can make.

**Targets worth aiming for:**

- **During business hours:** respond within 5–15 minutes
- **After hours:** an instant automated acknowledgment, then a real response first thing in the morning

**How to actually hit that:**

1. **Route forms to a phone, not just an inbox.** Get form submissions as texts or push notifications to whoever's on duty.
2. **Set up an instant auto-reply.** Every form submission should trigger an immediate text or email: *"Thanks for reaching out — we got your request and will call you within the hour / first thing tomorrow morning."* This alone keeps a lead from calling your competitor while they wait.
3. **Assign one owner per lead.** "Everyone checks the inbox" means nobody does. One person owns each inquiry until it's booked or dead.

## The Core Follow-Up Sequence

Here's a proven rhythm for a new inbound lead. Adjust the timing to your trade — emergency services compress this to minutes; high-ticket remodels stretch it to days.

**Touch 1 — Immediate (0–15 min):** Call first. If no answer, leave a short voicemail, then send a text:

> "Hi [Name], this is [You] from [Company] — got your request about [project]. Just tried your line. When's a good time to chat for 5 minutes? — [Phone]"

Text matters. Many homeowners won't answer an unknown number but will reply to a text within minutes.

**Touch 2 — Same day (2–4 hours later):** Second call attempt. If no answer, a brief email with your name, photo or logo, one line about the project they asked about, and two suggested call times.

**Touch 3 — Next morning:** Call again at a different time of day. People have patterns — if they missed you at 10 a.m., try 6 p.m.

**Touch 4 — Day 3:** The value-add message. Instead of "just checking in," give something:

> "Hi [Name] — while you're deciding, here's a quick guide on what [a water heater replacement / roof repair] typically costs and what affects the price: [link]. Happy to walk you through it."

**Touch 5 — Day 7:** Final attempt, framed honestly:

> "Hi [Name], I don't want to pester you — should I close your file, or would you like to keep talking about the [project]? Either answer is fine."

The "permission to say no" message gets a surprisingly high response rate because it removes pressure. Silence, "yes," or "not now" all give you clarity.

**After 5 touches:** Move them to the long-term nurture list (more below) rather than deleting them.

## Following Up on Estimates: The Most Neglected Money

New leads get attention; estimates get sent into the void. Yet an open estimate is your warmest possible prospect — they already chose you enough to invite you in.

**The estimate sequence:**

- **Day 0:** Deliver the estimate in person or on a call when possible, not as a cold PDF. Walk them through it. Estimates explained beat estimates emailed.
- **Day 2–3:** *"Any questions on the proposal? Happy to adjust scope or walk through options."*
- **Day 7:** Add value — a photo of a similar finished project, or a note addressing the concern they raised ("You mentioned timing — here's what our schedule looks like").
- **Day 14:** The honest close: *"Should I keep this on the books or release the slot?"* Scarcity and closure prompt decisions.
- **Monthly after that:** They join the nurture list. Remodel and replacement projects routinely close months after the first quote.

Many contractors find that simply adding the Day 2–3 and Day 14 touches — which take five minutes total — measurably lifts their close rate, because most competitors never do either.

## Scripts That Don't Sound Scripted

Follow-up fails when it sounds like a telemarketer. Three rules:

1. **Reference their specific project every time.** "Your bathroom remodel" not "your inquiry."
2. **Give a reason for the touch.** A question, a photo, scheduling info — never bare "checking in."
3. **Make replying effortless.** Ask one simple question per message.

**Texts that work:**

- *"Quick question so I can get you an accurate number — is the [unit/room] on the first floor or second?"*
- *"We had a cancellation Thursday if you want the earlier slot."*
- *"Still interested, or should I close this out? Either is fine!"*

Keep every message under about 40 words. Long messages get "I'll read this later" — which means never.

## The Long-Term Nurture List

The leads who said "not now" — and the estimates that went quiet — are not dead. They're delayed. Put them on a simple monthly email:

- One recent project photo with a two-line story
- One useful tip (seasonal maintenance, a cost guide)
- One soft invitation: "Reply to this email if you're ready to talk"

A plain-text-feeling email from the owner outperforms a glossy newsletter for local services. This list quietly produces some of your cheapest jobs, and it compounds every month you add to it.

## The System Behind the Sequence

Sequences fail without plumbing. Minimum viable setup:

- **One place where every lead lives** — a simple CRM, a shared spreadsheet, or even a whiteboard. Columns: name, contact info, project, stage, next touch date.
- **A daily 15-minute follow-up block** on someone's calendar, every day, no exceptions.
- **Templates for every message above** saved where whoever follows up can copy-paste and personalize in 30 seconds.
- **A weekly review:** how many new leads, how many touched 5 times, how many open estimates older than 7 days. What gets reviewed gets done.

Fancy automation is optional. Consistency is not.

## Measuring Whether It's Working

Track four numbers monthly:

1. **Response time** — average minutes from inquiry to first human response
2. **Contact rate** — what share of leads you actually reach
3. **Estimate-to-booking rate** — your close rate
4. **Booked jobs from touches 3+** — revenue that only exists because you followed up

That fourth number is the proof. Once owners see how much of their revenue comes from touches they used to skip, follow-up stops being a chore and starts being a priority.

## Quick Checklist

- [ ] Form submissions arrive as instant notifications to a phone
- [ ] Every inquiry gets an automatic immediate acknowledgment
- [ ] One named person owns each lead until it's resolved
- [ ] New leads get a call within 15 minutes during business hours
- [ ] Every lead gets 5 touches over 7 days: call, text, email, value-add, honest close
- [ ] Every estimate gets followed up at day 2–3 and day 14
- [ ] Templates for all messages are saved and ready to personalize
- [ ] "Not now" leads go on a monthly nurture email, not in the trash
- [ ] Follow-up time is blocked on a calendar daily
- [ ] Response time, contact rate, and close rate are reviewed monthly

The leads you're already paying for — through ads, SEO, and referrals — are worth more than the next leads you could buy. Follow up like it, and the bookings follow.
