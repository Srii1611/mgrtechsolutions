The feast-or-famine cycle is the biggest frustration for most handyman businesses: slammed one month, scrambling the next. The fix isn't luck or a bigger ad budget — it's building a handful of local visibility systems that keep your name in front of homeowners in your service area every single week. This guide covers exactly which marketing moves work for handyman businesses, in the order you should do them.

## Why Handyman Marketing Is Its Own Game

Before diving into tactics, understand what makes your situation unique:

- **You do dozens of job types.** A plumber markets one thing. You market everything from drywall patches to fence repairs — which makes keyword strategy trickier but also opens far more doors.
- **Jobs are small and fast.** You don't get weeks of customer research like a remodeler. People often hire the first credible handyman who answers the phone.
- **Repeat customers are your goldmine.** A homeowner who trusts you for one repair will call you for the next ten. Marketing's real job is starting relationships, not just landing one-off jobs.
- **Local trust matters enormously.** You're entering people's homes. Reviews, photos of you and your branded vehicle, and a professional online presence calm the "stranger in my house" anxiety.

## Step 1: Own the Google Map Pack

For handyman services, the Google local pack (the map with three listings) drives more calls than anything else. Here's how to earn a spot:

### Perfect Your Google Business Profile

- Choose "Handyman" as your primary category; add secondary categories for your most profitable services (e.g., "Dry wall contractor," "Painter," "Deck builder").
- Define your service area honestly — list the towns you'll actually drive to.
- Write a business description packed with your services and locations, in natural language: "Serving [Town] and [County] with drywall repair, door installation, deck repair, fixture replacement, and general home repairs."
- Add photos weekly: your work, your van, you on the job. Profiles with regular photo additions tend to outperform stale ones.
- Post a weekly update — a recent job, a seasonal reminder ("Time to winterize outdoor faucets"), or an availability note.

### Collect Reviews Relentlessly

Reviews are the #1 trust signal for someone inviting a stranger into their home. Many handymen find that calls jump noticeably once they pass 25–30 reviews.

- Text every customer a direct review link the same day you finish the job.
- Ask in person first — "Would you mind leaving me a quick Google review? It really helps my small business" — then send the link.
- Respond to every review. Thank the good ones; address bad ones calmly and professionally.

## Step 2: Build a Simple Website That Converts

You don't need a fancy site. You need one that answers three questions fast: What do you do? Where do you work? How do I hire you?

### Must-Have Pages

- **Homepage:** Clear headline ("Handyman Services in [Town]"), your service list, photos of your work, review highlights, and a prominent phone number plus a short contact form.
- **Services page (or pages):** List everything you do — this captures long-tail searches like "door hinge repair near me" and "gutter cleaning [Town]." Group them: drywall & painting, carpentry, plumbing basics, electrical basics, exterior work, odd jobs.
- **Service area page:** List every town you cover. Consider a dedicated page for your top two or three towns.
- **Gallery:** Before-and-after photos. Even small jobs — a patched hole, a re-hung door — show competence.
- **About page:** Your photo, your story, your credentials (licensed/insured where applicable). For a one-person business, personality is a selling point.

### Non-Negotiables

- Your phone number must be tap-to-call and visible on every page — most handyman leads come from phones.
- The site must load fast and look right on mobile.
- Your business name, address, and phone number must match your Google profile exactly.

## Step 3: Get Listed Where Homeowners Actually Look

Beyond Google, claim free profiles on:

- **Facebook Business Page** — post weekly job photos; join local community groups (follow each group's promo rules)
- **Yelp** — still drives real leads in many markets
- **Nextdoor** — hugely underrated for handymen; neighbors constantly ask for recommendations there
- **Bing Places** — takes 10 minutes, low competition
- **Local directories** — your town's chamber of commerce and any regional directories

Keep your business info identical on every listing. Inconsistencies hurt your Google rankings.

## Step 4: Turn Every Customer Into Three More

The cheapest marketing you'll ever do is maximizing each happy customer:

- **Ask for the next job before you leave.** "Anything else on your list while I'm here?" Unsold small jobs are everywhere.
- **Leave behind something physical.** A fridge magnet or business card stack beats a memory. When their faucet drips in six months, you want your name in the kitchen.
- **Ask for referrals directly.** "If any neighbors need work done, I'd appreciate you passing my name along." Then make it easy — a small referral discount or gift card works wonders.
- **Follow up in 6 months.** A text — "Hi [Name], it's [You], patched your drywall in March. Anything on the to-do list I can knock out?" — generates jobs from a warm list at zero cost.

## Step 5: Plant Long-Term Seeds with Content

You don't need to blog daily. But a few targeted pages can bring steady leads for years. Write one page answering each of these, with your town name worked in naturally:

- "How much does a handyman charge in [Town]?" (Be honest about your rates or minimums — it filters out mismatched customers.)
- "What jobs can a handyman legally do vs. when you need a licensed contractor?" (A huge trust-builder.)
- "10 small repairs that prevent big home problems"
- Seasonal lists: spring exterior fixes, fall weatherization, pre-holiday punch lists

One article a month is plenty. Each one is a permanent page that can rank and send you leads while you work.

## Step 6: Spend Money Only After the Free Stuff Works

Once your Google profile, reviews, and website are solid, consider:

- **Google Local Services Ads** — pay per lead, not per click, with a "Google Screened" badge. Often the best first paid channel for handymen.
- **Yard signs** — old-school but effective. With permission, leave a sign at every job for a week.
- **Vehicle branding** — your van or truck is a rolling billboard. A clear magnet or wrap pays for itself.
- **Avoid:** expensive broad Google Ads campaigns until you're tracking which channels actually produce calls.

## Your Weekly 60-Minute Marketing Routine

- **After every job (5 min):** Snap photos, text the review link.
- **Monday (20 min):** Post a job photo or tip to Google Business Profile and Facebook.
- **Friday (30 min):** Add photos to your website gallery; draft or publish one content piece per month; check your map ranking for "handyman near me."

Consistency over six months is what separates the handyman with a waitlist from the one refreshing job boards.

## Quick Checklist

- [ ] Google Business Profile claimed, categorized, and updated weekly with photos
- [ ] Review request texted to every customer the day the job finishes
- [ ] Website has tap-to-call number, full service list, service area page, and gallery
- [ ] Business info identical across Google, Facebook, Yelp, Nextdoor, and Bing
- [ ] Nextdoor presence established and local Facebook groups joined
- [ ] Fridge magnets or cards left at every job
- [ ] 6-month follow-up text system in place for past customers
- [ ] One locally-focused article published per month
- [ ] Referral ask (with a small thank-you) part of every job wrap-up
- [ ] Paid ads considered only after free channels are running
