Customers are starting to ask AI assistants — ChatGPT, Google's AI Overviews, voice assistants — for recommendations instead of scrolling through ten blue links. That shift doesn't mean your business becomes invisible, but it does mean the rules for getting found are evolving. This article gives you a practical, week-by-week 90-day plan to position your local business so AI-powered search works for you instead of against you.

## First, Understand What Actually Changed

Here's the honest picture: AI search tools still pull from the same underlying web. They favor businesses that are well-documented, well-reviewed, and clearly described across the internet. The fundamentals of local marketing haven't been thrown out — but their importance has shifted.

What AI assistants tend to rely on when recommending a local business:

- **Your Google Business Profile** and the reviews attached to it
- **Your website content**, especially clear service descriptions and FAQ-style answers
- **Mentions of your business across the web** — directories, local news, industry sites, review platforms
- **Consistency** — whether your name, services, and location line up everywhere

What matters less in an AI-answer world: clever keyword tricks, thin location pages, and content written for algorithms instead of humans. The 90-day plan below focuses on the things that move the needle.

## Days 1–30: Lock Down Your Foundation

### Week 1: Audit how you look to a machine

Before changing anything, see your business the way AI does:

- Search your business name on Google. Is the information accurate and complete?
- Ask an AI assistant "Who's the best [your service] in [your town]?" and "Tell me about [your business name]." Note what it says — and where it seems to be getting its information.
- Google your business name plus "reviews" and see which platforms come up.
- Check that your name, address, and phone number are identical on your website, Google profile, Facebook, Yelp, and any directory listings.

Write down every inconsistency you find. This audit becomes your to-do list.

### Week 2: Rebuild your Google Business Profile as a data source

Treat your profile less like a listing and more like a structured database AI tools read:

- Fill out every service with its own description written in plain language.
- Add and answer questions in the Q&A section yourself — the ten questions customers ask most on the phone.
- Upload recent, real photos of your team, trucks, and completed work.
- Make sure hours, service area, and attributes are current.

### Week 3: Make your website answer questions directly

AI tools reward content that plainly answers the questions people ask. Add or improve:

- **A detailed FAQ page** covering pricing factors, service area, timelines, warranties, licensing, and your process. Write questions the way customers actually phrase them.
- **Individual service pages** that clearly state what each service includes, who it's for, and what it roughly costs or what affects the cost.
- **An "About" page** that says who you are, how long you've served the area, and what makes your approach different. AI tools look for credibility signals like experience and location.

Write like you talk. "How much does it cost to replace a water heater in Springfield?" followed by an honest, helpful paragraph beats a page of keyword-stuffed fluff every time.

### Week 4: Fix your citations

Get your business listed — accurately — on the major data sources: the big directories, your local chamber of commerce, and any trade-specific directories for your industry. Consistency matters more than volume. Twenty accurate listings beat fifty sloppy ones.

## Days 31–60: Build the Proof Layer

AI assistants are cautious about recommending businesses. They lean on evidence that other humans trust you.

### Week 5–6: Run a review sprint

Reviews are arguably the single biggest input AI tools use to judge local businesses:

- Set a goal to meaningfully grow your Google review count over these two weeks through a systematic ask — text every happy customer a direct link.
- Don't ignore other platforms entirely; reviews on a couple of relevant sites (Yelp, Facebook, or an industry platform) add breadth.
- Respond to every review. Review responses are content too — they show engagement and add descriptive detail about your services.

### Week 7: Publish genuinely helpful content

Start a simple habit: one useful article or page per week answering a real customer question. Good topics for AI-era content:

- Cost explainers ("What affects the price of a roof replacement?")
- Decision guides ("Repair vs. replace: how to decide")
- Local angle ("How [your state's] weather affects your HVAC system")

This isn't about gaming algorithms. AI tools summarize the web — the more clearly and helpfully your site explains your field, the more likely it becomes a source worth citing.

### Week 8: Get mentioned locally

AI recommendations lean on the broader web's picture of you. Earn a few legitimate mentions:

- Sponsor a local team or event (these often come with website links).
- Reach out to local news or community blogs with a genuinely interesting angle.
- Partner with complementary businesses and link to each other.
- Join your chamber or trade association and make sure your member profile links to your site.

## Days 61–90: Adapt and Measure

### Week 9: Structure your content for extraction

Make it easy for AI to pull accurate facts from your site:

- Use clear headings that state the question, followed by a direct answer in the first sentence.
- Keep key facts — services, service area, credentials — in text, not buried in images.
- Add basic structured data (LocalBusiness and FAQ schema) to your site. If you have a web person, this is a quick job; it labels your content so machines read it correctly.

### Week 10: Test your AI visibility again

Repeat your Day 1 audit. Ask the same AI assistants the same questions. What's changed? Where is information still wrong or missing? Each gap points to a place on the web that needs better information — usually your own site or a key listing.

### Week 11: Strengthen your direct channels

Here's the strategic truth behind all of this: the businesses most insulated from any search shift are the ones customers contact directly. Use this week to:

- Make sure every customer gets onto your email or text list.
- Set up a quarterly follow-up message to past customers.
- Sharpen your referral ask.

AI search may change how strangers find you; it can't change how past customers feel about you.

### Week 12: Set your ongoing rhythm

Turn the sprint into a sustainable routine:

- **Weekly:** ask for reviews, answer one customer question on your site or social channels.
- **Monthly:** check your Google profile, post photos, review where leads came from.
- **Quarterly:** repeat the AI visibility audit and fix any gaps.

## What Not to Do

- **Don't chase "AI optimization" gimmicks.** Anyone selling a magic formula for ranking in ChatGPT is selling vapor. The inputs are the ones above.
- **Don't abandon Google.** Traditional search still drives the majority of local leads today, and AI tools lean heavily on Google's ecosystem anyway.
- **Don't publish thin AI-generated content at scale.** Mass-produced generic pages are exactly what search systems — AI or otherwise — are learning to filter out.

- [ ] Create or improve individual pages for each service
- [ ] Clean up your top 20 directory listings
- [ ] Run a two-week review generation sprint
- [ ] Publish one genuinely helpful article per week
- [ ] Earn 3–5 local mentions, links, or sponsorships
