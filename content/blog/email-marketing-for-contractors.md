Somewhere in your filing cabinet, your old invoices, or that spreadsheet you haven't opened in a year, there's a list of people who already hired you once — or almost did. That list is worth more than most of the advertising you could buy, because these folks already know your name and your work. Email is the cheapest, simplest way to wake that list up. Here's how to do it without feeling like a spammer or needing a marketing degree.

## Why Email Works So Well for Contractors

Most of your marketing fights for the attention of strangers. Email does the opposite: it talks to people who already raised their hand once.

Three things make it especially powerful for trade businesses:

- **Your work is repeatable and cyclical.** HVAC systems need seasonal tune-ups, decks need resealing, water heaters age out, paint fades. Past customers will need you again — the only question is whether they remember your name when the moment comes.
- **Old quotes are buried treasure.** Many contractors have dozens or hundreds of estimates that never closed. A meaningful share of those people simply got busy, got cold feet on price, or hired nobody at all. They're not lost — they're dormant.
- **It costs almost nothing.** Email tools run free to about $30 a month at contractor scale. No ad auctions, no pay-per-click. Many businesses find that a single revived job pays for years of the software.

## Step 1: Round Up Your List (Legally and Sensibly)

Before you send anything, you need one clean list. Gather contacts from:

- Past invoices and your job-management or accounting software
- Old estimates that never closed
- Business cards and paper intake forms
- Your phone's contacts and email history
- Warranty and maintenance records

Put them into a simple spreadsheet: first name, email, what job or quote it was, roughly when. Don't obsess over perfection — "Mary, kitchen remodel quote, 2023" is plenty.

**A quick word on permission:** emailing your actual past customers about your services is normal business practice and generally fine. For old leads who never hired you, keep it respectful: a personal-feeling reintroduction, a clear reason you're writing, and an easy way to say no thanks. Every bulk email you send must include a working unsubscribe link — any reputable email tool handles this automatically, and it's the law in most places. Never buy a list of strangers; it torches your sender reputation and your local reputation.

## Step 2: Pick a Simple Tool

You don't need enterprise software. Tools like Mailchimp, Brevo, MailerLite, or Constant Contact all handle small contractor lists fine, and most have free tiers for a few hundred contacts. What matters:

- Easy signup forms and unsubscribe handling (built in)
- Basic open/click reports so you can see what works
- Simple templates that look decent on a phone

Spend an hour setting it up, import your spreadsheet, and you're ready. If even that feels like too much, you can start with plain personal emails from your normal inbox to old leads — more on that below.

## Step 3: The Three Campaigns That Actually Book Jobs

You don't need a clever newsletter strategy. Start with these three proven email types.

### 1. The "Win-Back" Email to Dead Leads

This is the highest-value email most contractors never send. Go through quotes from 3 to 18 months ago that never closed, and send a short, personal note:

> Subject: Still thinking about the bathroom remodel?
>
> Hi Tom — we quoted your bathroom remodel last spring and I know the timing wasn't right. We're booking projects for next month and I wanted to check in before the schedule fills. Happy to take another look or refresh the numbers if anything's changed. — Dave

No graphics, no discount screaming, no corporate tone. It reads like a person, because it is. Send these individually or in small batches from your own address if the list is small. Contractors are routinely surprised when one or two of every ten old quotes comes back to life.

### 2. The Seasonal Reminder to Past Customers

Trade work follows the calendar, so let the calendar write your emails:

- **HVAC:** pre-summer cooling checks, pre-winter furnace tune-ups
- **Roofing/gutters:** post-storm inspections, fall gutter cleaning
- **Plumbing:** winterization reminders, water heater checkups
- **Painting:** spring exterior booking windows
- **Electrical:** pre-holiday lighting and panel checks

A simple template: one sentence about the season, one sentence about why acting now helps, one clear call to action ("Reply to this email or call to get on the schedule"). Send two to four of these a year, per season relevant to your trade.

### 3. The Quarterly "Stay in Touch" Email

Every two or three months, send your whole list something light:

- A recent project with a before-and-after photo
- A seasonal home maintenance tip
- A note that referral season is open, with a small thank-you offer

The goal isn't to sell every time — it's to make sure that when their neighbor asks "know a good electrician?", your name is the one that surfaces.

## Step 4: Write Emails People Actually Open

A few rules that matter more than design:

- **Subject lines should sound like a person wrote them.** "Quick question about your furnace" beats "SPRING SAVINGS SPECTACULAR!!!" every time.
- **One idea per email.** Don't stack three offers and four links. Pick one thing you want them to do.
- **Write like you talk.** If you wouldn't say it across a kitchen table, don't write it.
- **Make it mobile-friendly.** Most of your customers will read it on a phone. Short paragraphs, one button or a plain "just reply" ask.
- **Sign as a human.** "Mike, Mike's Plumbing" with a phone number. Not "The Team at Mike's Plumbing Solutions LLC."

## Step 5: Measure the Only Numbers That Matter

Ignore the fancy dashboards. Track three things:

1. **Replies and calls.** The real metric. Did anyone book?
2. **Opens.** If fewer than roughly a quarter of your list opens, your subject lines need work or your list is stale.
3. **Unsubscribes.** A few per campaign is normal and healthy. A flood means you're emailing too often or too salesy — dial it back.

After each send, note which email produced jobs. Within a few campaigns you'll know exactly what your list responds to.

## Common Mistakes That Kill Contractor Email

- **Blasting the whole list constantly.** Once or twice a month maximum; quarterly is fine.
- **Only emailing when you're desperate for work.** Customers can smell it, and the pattern trains them to ignore you. Steady beats sporadic.
- **No call to action.** Every email should end with one clear next step: reply, call, or book.
- **Giving up after one send.** The first email to a cold list often does the worst. Consistency over three to six months is where the payoff shows up.
- **Buying lists.** Worth repeating: don't. Ever.

## A Realistic First Month

Week 1: gather your list into a spreadsheet and pick a tool. Week 2: send win-back emails to your ten best old quotes. Week 3: draft your first seasonal reminder and send it to past customers. Week 4: put the next three seasonal sends on the calendar. Total time invested: maybe four hours. That's the entire system.

## Quick Checklist

- [ ] I've pulled past customers and old quotes into one spreadsheet
- [ ] I've chosen an email tool with unsubscribe handling built in
- [ ] I've sent (or scheduled) personal win-back emails to my 10 best dead leads
- [ ] I've drafted one seasonal reminder email for my trade
- [ ] Every email has one clear call to action and sounds like a person wrote it
- [ ] I've set a quarterly stay-in-touch email on the calendar
- [ ] I know the three numbers I'll track: replies, opens, unsubscribes
