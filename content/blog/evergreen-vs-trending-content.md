You've been told you should "post content" for your business. But what kind? One expert says chase trends and hop on viral topics; another says write timeless guides that rank on Google for years. Both are half right — and if you pick the wrong mix, you'll burn hours on content that never brings in a single customer. Here's how evergreen and trending content actually work for a local service business, and a practical plan for balancing them.

## The Two Types, in Plain English

**Evergreen content** stays useful for years. It's the answer to a question people will still be asking in five years: "Why is my water heater making a knocking sound?" "How much does a roof replacement cost?" "How often should I service my AC?" It works like a slow-cooker meal — quiet effort up front, value for a long time.

**Trending content** rides what's happening right now: a hailstorm that just hit your county, a new energy rebate program, a sudden cold snap, a regulation change, a viral home-maintenance hack. It's a flash fry — intense, fast, and over quickly.

The core trade-off:

| | Evergreen | Trending |
|---|---|---|
| Lifespan | Years | Days to weeks |
| Traffic pattern | Slow build, steady drip | Sharp spike, then gone |
| Where it wins | Google search, your website | Social media, email, local groups |
| Effort | Higher upfront | Lower per piece, but constant |
| Risk | Takes months to rank | Miss the moment and it's worthless |

## Why Evergreen Content Is Your Foundation

For a local service business, evergreen content does something advertising can't: it keeps working while you're on a job.

A homeowner whose toilet is gurgling at 10pm searches "why is my toilet gurgling." If your plumbing company published a clear, helpful article answering exactly that — with your name, your town, and your phone number attached — you've got a warm lead delivered by work you did months or years ago. That article doesn't clock out, doesn't charge per click, and doesn't stop when your ad budget runs dry.

Evergreen topics worth writing for almost any trade:

- **Cost questions:** "How much does [service] cost in [your area]?" Be honest about ranges and what drives the price up or down.
- **Problem diagnosis:** "Why does my [furnace/roof/drain] do [symptom]?"
- **Repair vs. replace:** "Is it worth fixing a 15-year-old AC unit?"
- **How-to-choose guides:** "What to look for when hiring a [your trade]."
- **Seasonal prep:** "How to get your home ready for winter" — timeless even though it's seasonal, because it returns every year.
- **Local angle:** "Do I need a permit for [project] in [city]?" Local specificity is where small businesses beat national websites.

The catch: evergreen content is a long game. Many businesses find it takes three to nine months for a new article to start pulling steady search traffic. That's normal. You're building an asset, not buying an ad — and like any asset, it compounds.

## Where Trending Content Earns Its Keep

If evergreen is your foundation, trending content is your radar — it grabs attention *now* and proves you're an active, plugged-in local business.

Trending content shines in three situations:

**1. Weather and local events.** A hailstorm, deep freeze, flood, or heatwave puts your services top of mind within hours. The roofer who posts "what to do in the first 24 hours after hail damage" the morning after a storm — in local Facebook groups, on Google Business Profile, in a quick email to past customers — will field calls while competitors are still having coffee. Speed matters more than polish here.

**2. News your customers care about.** New rebates for heat pumps, changes to local building codes, a recall on a common appliance part. Translating dry news into "here's what this means for your house" positions you as the helpful expert.

**3. Social media reach.** Algorithms reward timeliness. A quick phone video reacting to a viral "hack" ("Here's why that TikTok drain trick can crack your pipes") gets more reach in a day than your best article gets in a month.

The limits are real, though. Trending content decays fast — that storm post is irrelevant in two weeks. And chasing every trend turns marketing into a treadmill. Use trends as seasoning, not the meal.

## The 80/20 Mix That Works for Local Businesses

For most local service businesses, a sensible starting split is roughly 80% evergreen, 20% trending. Here's why: evergreen builds the durable search presence that brings leads month after month, while trending gives you periodic bursts of local visibility and keeps your channels from looking abandoned.

In practice, that might look like:

- **One evergreen article per month** on your website, answering a real customer question.
- **A handful of quick trending posts** — social updates, Google Business Profile posts, short emails — whenever something timely hits your service area.

One rule keeps you sane: **evergreen is scheduled, trending is reactive.** Put your evergreen articles on a calendar and produce them steadily, rain or shine. Only make trending content when there's a genuine moment — a storm, a rebate, a seasonal spike. Don't manufacture trends to fill a quota.

## Where Each Type Should Live

- **Evergreen → your website.** Search engines are where timeless questions get asked. A well-organized blog or learning center on your own site builds an asset you own (unlike social followers, which you're only renting).
- **Trending → social media, Google Business Profile posts, and email.** These channels reward speed and recency, and your existing followers and past customers are the people most likely to respond to timely news.
- **The bridge move:** when a trend hits, link it to your evergreen content. Posting about the hailstorm? Link to your "how to choose a roofer" guide. The spike in attention spills over onto your permanent assets.

## A Simple System for Never Running Out of Ideas

**For evergreen topics, mine your own phone calls.** Every question a customer asks you is an article. "Do I really need to replace the whole panel?" "Can you patch this or does the whole wall need redoing?" Keep a note on your phone; add every question you hear twice. That's your editorial calendar, written by your actual customers.

**For trending topics, set two tripwires:**

1. **Weather awareness.** Watch the forecast the way you already do for scheduling — but now with a marketing eye. Big weather coming? Draft your post *before* it hits so you can publish within hours.
2. **One news source.** Follow your trade association, local news, or manufacturer updates — just one source, checked weekly. When something affects homeowners, translate it into plain English.

Batch your work: write two evergreen articles in one sitting when business is slow, and keep a template ready for trending posts (headline, what happened, what to do now, call us) so a reactive post takes 20 minutes, not two hours.

## Mistakes to Avoid

- **Chasing trends at the expense of foundations.** A viral post feels great and feeds nothing next month. Never skip the evergreen work for the sugar rush.
- **Writing generic content with no local angle.** "5 Plumbing Tips" competes with the entire internet. "Why [Town]'s hard water is murdering your water heater" competes with nobody.
- **Publishing and abandoning.** Refresh your top evergreen articles once a year — update prices, photos, and dates. Ten minutes of upkeep keeps them ranking.
- **Expecting instant results.** Give evergreen content six months before judging it. Judge trending content in six days.

- [ ] I've listed 10 real customer questions to use as evergreen article topics
- [ ] I've committed to a steady evergreen schedule (e.g., one article per month) on my own website
- [ ] I've set up my trending "tripwires": weather watch + one industry news source
- [ ] I have a 20-minute template ready for reactive posts (what happened / what to do / call us)
- [ ] My split is roughly 80% evergreen, 20% trending
- [ ] When I post trending content, I link it to a related evergreen page
- [ ] I've scheduled a yearly refresh for my top-performing evergreen articles
