Ask ten local business owners about Google Business Profile posts and you'll get the same answer: "I did a few when I set up the profile, then... life happened." The problem isn't motivation — it's that nobody hands you a realistic plan. Posting daily is fantasy for a business where you're also answering phones and running jobs. But one thoughtful post per week? That's about fifteen minutes, and it's enough to keep your profile visibly active, give customers fresh reasons to choose you, and give Google ongoing signals that your business is alive. Here's the complete system: what to post, when, and a rotating weekly plan you can run forever without repeating yourself.

## Why Weekly Posts Are Worth Fifteen Minutes

Let's be honest about what GBP posts do and don't do. They're not going to double your calls overnight, and their direct ranking influence is modest. What they actually deliver:

- **An active-looking profile.** When a customer compares you to a competitor, recent posts signal a thriving business. A profile whose last update was two years ago raises quiet doubts.
- **More reasons to call.** Posts with offers, seasonal reminders, and proof of recent work give searchers a nudge at the exact decision moment.
- **Engagement signals.** Activity on your profile — clicks, calls, post views — correlates with better local visibility over time.
- **Free advertising space.** Posts appear on your profile in search and Maps. That's prime real estate, and it costs nothing.

The businesses that benefit most are the ones that treat posting like brushing their teeth: small, routine, never skipped.

## Know Your Post Types

Google offers a few post formats. Each has a best use:

| Post Type | Best For | Key Feature |
|-----------|----------|-------------|
| **Update** | General news, recent work, tips | Flexible; photo + text + button |
| **Offer** | Promotions, seasonal discounts | Start/end dates, coupon codes, terms |
| **Event** | Open houses, community events, workshops | Date and time fields |

A few ground rules that apply to all of them:

- **Posts fade.** Older posts get pushed down, which is exactly why consistency matters more than any single post.
- **Always include a photo.** Posts with real images dramatically outperform text-only ones. Use your job photos, not stock.
- **Use the button.** Every post can have a call-to-action button — "Call now," "Learn more," "Book." Link it to the most relevant page on your site, not just your homepage.
- **Keep text tight.** Write the important part in the first sentence or two — that's what shows before the "read more" cut.
- **No phone-number-only posts or keyword stuffing.** Write like a human talking to a neighbor.

## The 8-Week Rotating Posting Calendar

Here's the heart of the system: eight post themes that rotate, so you never stare at a blank screen wondering what to write. After eight weeks, the cycle repeats with fresh examples. Adjust the order to fit your trade and seasons.

**Week 1 — Recent Job Spotlight**
Showcase a completed project: what the customer needed, what you did, and a photo of the result. "Just wrapped up a full water heater replacement for a family in [neighborhood] — same-day service, hot showers by dinner." These posts double as proof and local relevance.

**Week 2 — Seasonal Tip**
Share one genuinely useful piece of advice tied to the season: prepping AC before summer, protecting pipes before a freeze, gutter cleaning before the rains. Educational posts build trust and get saved and shared.

**Week 3 — Offer or Promotion**
Run a real offer with the Offer post type: a seasonal discount, a free estimate, a maintenance special. Include an end date — legitimate urgency works, and Google displays the dates.

**Week 4 — Review Highlight**
Screenshot-free version: quote a recent five-star review (paraphrase or trim it), thank the customer, and invite others to share their experience. This nudges more reviews while showcasing the ones you have.

**Week 5 — Meet the Team**
Introduce a crew member with a photo: their role, how long they've been with you, something human. Customers hire people — these posts quietly build comfort before you ever knock on their door.

**Week 6 — FAQ Answer**
Take one question you hear constantly — "Do you charge for estimates?" "How long does a roof replacement take?" — and answer it in three or four sentences. Position yourself as the helpful expert.

**Week 7 — Before and After**
Your most persuasive content. Describe the transformation briefly and let the photos do the work. (You're already taking these on every job, right?)

**Week 8 — Community or Company News**
Sponsoring a little league team? Hired a new tech? Celebrating ten years in business? Attended a training or earned a certification? Local, human news rounds out your presence.

Then the rotation restarts — eight weeks later you have new jobs, new reviews, new seasons, so nothing ever repeats verbatim.

## Adapting the Calendar to Your Season

Every local trade has a rhythm. Smart posting means shifting emphasis with demand:

- **Before your busy season:** push seasonal tips and pre-booking offers ("Schedule your AC tune-up before the first heat wave"). Capture demand early.
- **During peak season:** lean on job spotlights and before-and-afters — you're drowning in fresh material anyway.
- **In the slow season:** run your strongest offers, highlight reviews, and promote the services people forget about (maintenance plans, indoor projects, inspections).

Keep a simple note on your phone with the eight themes and whatever's coming next. Planning a month ahead takes ten minutes.

## A 15-Minute Weekly Workflow

Here's how to keep this from ever becoming a burden:

1. **Minute 0–3:** Check the rotation. Which theme is this week?
2. **Minute 3–8:** Pick a photo from the week's jobs and write two to four sentences. Plain, friendly, specific. Mention your city or neighborhood naturally.
3. **Minute 8–12:** Add the right button and link — your booking page, a service page, or "Call now."
4. **Minute 12–15:** Preview and publish. Done.

Batch option: if weekly feels choppy, sit down once a month and draft all four posts. Keep the photos and text in a folder, then publish each Monday. Some business management tools and GBP itself allow scheduling through connected platforms — use whatever keeps you consistent.

## What to Avoid

- **Disappearing for months.** A dead profile section is worse than a modest one. If you can't do weekly, do biweekly — but do it without fail.
- **Pure self-promotion, every time.** If every post is "Hire us! Best prices!" people tune out. The rotation above keeps selling to about a quarter of your posts.
- **Stock photos.** Real job photos only. Customers can smell stock imagery.
- **Overloaded posts.** One idea per post. A wall of text with three offers and five hashtags helps no one.
- **Ignoring the responses.** Occasionally customers comment or ask questions via your profile. Answer them.
- **Forgetting your other channels exist.** A GBP post takes almost no extra effort to adapt into a Facebook post. Write once, publish twice.

## How to Tell If It's Working

Check your profile's performance data monthly:

- **Calls and direction requests** — are they trending up over the quarter?
- **Website clicks** from your profile
- **Post views** — which themes get seen most? Do more of those.

Judge over quarters, not weeks. Posts compound: an active profile plus fresh photos plus growing reviews is what moves the needle, and posts are the thread that ties it together.

## Quick Checklist

- [ ] Commit to one post per week — put it on the calendar like an appointment
- [ ] Use the 8-week rotating theme plan so you never run out of ideas
- [ ] Attach a real job photo to every post
- [ ] Include a call-to-action button with a relevant link
- [ ] Put the key message in the first two sentences
- [ ] Use the Offer post type for promotions, with real dates
- [ ] Shift themes to match your busy and slow seasons
- [ ] Keep self-promotion to roughly one post in four
- [ ] Batch-draft a month of posts when you have a quiet hour
- [ ] Repurpose each post to Facebook for double the value
- [ ] Review profile performance monthly and do more of what gets views
