You've got a website. It looks fine. And it produces roughly zero leads — maybe a stray form submission every few weeks, while competitors seem to book jobs from theirs constantly. The instinct is to blame the whole thing and start shopping for a redesign. Sometimes that's right, but often the problem is one specific, fixable break in a chain — and replacing the whole chain when one link failed wastes money and months of time.

This playbook works through the lead-generation chain in order: **traffic → trust → conversion → follow-up**. Find where your chain breaks, fix that link, and measure again.

## Step 1: Diagnose Before You Fix Anything

Every "my website doesn't generate leads" problem is one of four root causes:

1. **Not enough visitors** (a traffic problem)
2. **The wrong visitors** (a targeting problem)
3. **Visitors who don't contact you** (a conversion problem)
4. **Contacts you never respond to** (a follow-up problem — more common than anyone admits)

Here's how to tell which one you have, using free tools:

- **Check your traffic** in Google Analytics (install it if you haven't). A local service site with under ~200 monthly visitors has a traffic problem; one with 500+ visitors and no leads has a conversion or follow-up problem.
- **Check where traffic comes from and lands.** If most visitors come from outside your service area or land on an old blog post about nothing you sell, you have a targeting problem.
- **Test your contact path yourself.** Submit your form, call your number. If the form never reaches you or the phone rings somewhere unmanned, you have a follow-up problem masquerading as a website problem.
- **Search like a customer.** Incognito window: "[your service] [your town]." If you're not on page one, lack of visibility is your starting point.

Diagnose honestly, then jump to the matching fix below.

## Fix 1: The Traffic Problem — Nobody's Finding You

If barely anyone visits, nothing else matters yet. The causes, and their cures:

**Your site has too few pages.** A five-page site (home, about, services, gallery, contact) gives Google almost nothing to rank. The fix: build one thorough page per service you offer and one per major town you serve. A plumber should have pages for water heater repair, drain cleaning, repiping, and fixture installation — each answering what it costs, what's involved, and when to call.

**Your Google Business Profile is neglected.** For local searches, the map pack drives a huge share of clicks, and it's powered by your profile, not your website. Complete every field, add photos monthly, post occasionally, and — most importantly — generate a steady stream of reviews. For many local businesses, an optimized profile produces leads before the website does.

**You have no reviews.** Reviews are both a ranking factor and the tiebreaker customers use. Build asking into every completed job, with a direct link sent by text the same day.

**You're impatient.** A new or rebuilt site takes months to climb. If your traffic foundation is new, keep building pages and reviews for six months before judging.

## Fix 2: The Targeting Problem — Wrong Visitors, Wrong Pages

Traffic that can't buy from you isn't a lead source. Common mismatches and cures:

**Blog posts attracting the whole country.** A post like "What is SEER rating?" can rank nationally and bring hundreds of visitors who live nowhere near you. That's not useless (it builds authority), but don't count it as a lead failure when they don't call. The fix: shift content toward locally relevant, decision-stage topics — "How much does AC replacement cost in [region]?" attracts people who can actually hire you.

**Service pages that never mention where you work.** If your water heater page doesn't name your city and service area, neither Google nor visitors know it's relevant locally. Weave your geography into every service page naturally.

**Ranking for informational searches only.** "How to unclog a drain" brings DIYers; "drain cleaning service [town]" brings customers. You want some of both, but your core pages must target the hiring phrases.

## Fix 3: The Conversion Problem — Visitors Who Don't Reach Out

This is where most lead-starved websites actually break. The visitor arrived; the site failed to close. Work through these causes:

**No clear next step.** Every page needs one obvious action: "Call now" or "Get a free estimate," in a button that stands out. If your homepage offers seven competing options, visitors choose none. One page, one job.

**The phone number isn't tappable or visible.** On a phone — where most of your visitors are — the number must be a tap-to-call button at the top of every page. Full stop.

**Forms that ask too much.** Name, phone, address or zip, and a one-line description. Every field beyond that costs you completions. And test the form yourself monthly — broken forms are epidemic.

**No trust signals near the ask.** Visitors decide in seconds whether you're legitimate. Put your Google rating, review count, license info, and real job photos right next to every call to action — not hidden on a testimonials page nobody opens.

**Slow load times.** Every extra second of load time sheds visitors before they ever see your offer. Compress images, ditch homepage sliders, and upgrade bargain hosting.

**Nothing addresses their real anxiety.** Service customers worry about price, trustworthiness, and hassle. Pages that acknowledge and answer these ("Here's exactly what a service call costs" / "Our techs are background-checked, uniformed, and on time") convert noticeably better than pages of generic capability claims.

## Fix 4: The Follow-Up Problem — Leads You're Actually Getting (and Losing)

Before blaming the website, audit what happens after someone reaches out. Many "website problems" are actually this:

- Form submissions sitting unread for hours or days — in local services, the first company to respond wins a disproportionate share of jobs
- Calls going to voicemail during business hours
- Quote requests answered with a slow, vague reply instead of a scheduled visit

The fixes are operational, not technical: route form alerts to a carried phone, set response-time standards (minutes, not days), and track every inquiry to an outcome. If your website generates five leads a month and you fumble three, fixing the website was never the priority.

## Your 90-Day Repair Plan

**Days 1–14: Stop the bleeding.** Test and fix forms, phone links, and response processes. Install analytics and call tracking so you can see reality.

**Days 15–45: Fix conversion.** Rewrite calls to action, add trust signals, compress images, simplify navigation, ensure service pages name your service area.

**Days 46–90: Build traffic.** Complete your Google Business Profile, launch a review request habit, and publish or improve one service or location page per week.

Then measure for a full quarter. Lead generation compounds: reviews feed rankings, rankings feed traffic, conversion improvements multiply whatever traffic you earn. Most sites that "don't work" are two or three disciplined fixes away from working — the trick is fixing the right link in the chain first.

## Quick Checklist

- [ ] Identified which of the four root causes (traffic, targeting, conversion, follow-up) is my real problem
- [ ] Installed analytics and call tracking to see actual visitor and lead numbers
- [ ] Tested my contact form and phone path end to end
- [ ] Built or improved one page per service and per major town
- [ ] Completed my Google Business Profile and started a review habit
- [ ] Placed one clear call to action with trust signals on every page
- [ ] Simplified forms to four fields or fewer
- [ ] Set a response-time standard of minutes, not hours
- [ ] Added my service area naturally to every service page
- [ ] Committed to a 90-day plan with a quarterly measurement date
