Fencing is a visual, high-trust, big-ticket sale. Homeowners spend weeks thinking about it, look at photos obsessively, and worry about hiring the wrong crew. That's actually good news for your marketing: the companies that show their work well and look trustworthy online win a disproportionate share of the jobs. Here's how to be one of them.

## Start With What Makes Fence Buyers Tick

Before spending a dollar on marketing, understand the customer's mindset. A fence buyer usually:

- Has a triggering event — a new dog, a new pool, a privacy problem, a storm that flattened the old fence
- Spends days or weeks researching styles, materials, and prices before contacting anyone
- Worries about three things: Will it look good? Will it last? Will this crew show up and finish?
- Gets two or three quotes and often picks the company that felt most trustworthy, not the cheapest

Every marketing decision below comes back to those four facts.

## Your Photos Are Your Best Salesperson

No trade benefits from good photography more than fencing. A clean cedar privacy fence or a crisp black aluminum run sells itself in a picture — and a blurry, crooked photo of the same fence sells nothing.

**Build the habit:**

- Photograph every completed job from several angles, in good light, with the homeowner's permission.
- Capture variety: wood, vinyl, chain link, ornamental iron; front yards, backyards, gates, and custom details.
- Take close-ups of craftsmanship — post caps, gate hardware, clean lines — not just wide shots.
- Keep photographing. A gallery that grows every week signals a busy, in-demand company.

These photos power everything else in this guide: your Google profile, your website, your social media, and your ads.

## Own Your Google Business Profile

When someone searches "fence company near me" or "fence installation [your town]," the map results are where the calls come from. Winning there is mostly about doing the basics better than competitors:

- Fill out every field: services (wood fence installation, vinyl, chain link, repairs, gates), service area, hours.
- Upload new job photos weekly — Google favors active profiles, and customers favor pretty ones.
- Ask every customer for a review at the moment they're happiest: the day the fence is done and they're standing in the yard admiring it. Text them the direct review link before you leave the driveway.
- Answer every question posted to your profile and respond to every review.

For most fence companies, the combination of a strong profile, fifty-plus reviews, and a big photo gallery produces more leads than everything else combined — and it's free.

## A Website That Handles the Research Phase

Because fence buyers research heavily before calling, your website should act like a helpful salesperson for the person browsing at 10 p.m.:

- **A gallery page with lots of photos**, organized by material if possible.
- **A page per material and style** — wood, vinyl, chain link, aluminum — covering pros, cons, maintenance, and what affects the cost.
- **Honest pricing guidance.** You don't need exact numbers; a page explaining "what a fence costs and why" (linear footage, terrain, gates, old fence removal) builds enormous trust and filters out sticker-shock leads.
- **The practical stuff people ask about:** Do you handle permits? Do you call 811 for utility locates? What about property lines and HOAs? Answering these marks you as a pro.
- **Real proof:** reviews, photos, years in business, license and insurance.
- **A quote request form** that asks for the property address and rough footage, so you can respond with something useful instead of just "call us."

## Reviews and Referrals: Your Compounding Engine

Fences are seen by the whole neighborhood, which makes fencing uniquely referral-friendly.

- **Yard signs.** Ask every customer if you can leave a sign for a couple of weeks. Neighbors considering a fence will walk over and look at yours up close. This is one of the cheapest, highest-return tactics in the trade.
- **Talk to the neighbors.** When you're installing, a friendly note or knock at adjacent homes — "We're building the Johnsons' fence this week; if you've been thinking about yours, we're happy to swing by for a free quote" — wins jobs regularly.
- **Ask for referrals explicitly.** Past customers with a two-year-old fence are happy to recommend you if you remind them you exist. A yearly check-in text or postcard keeps you top of mind.
- **Partner with adjacent pros.** Landscapers, pool builders, deck contractors, realtors, and property managers all meet homeowners who need fences. Build a short list of partners who send work back and forth.

## Social Media: Simple and Visual Wins

You don't need to be everywhere. For fence companies, two channels pull most of the weight:

- **Facebook:** post photos of finished jobs with the town named ("6-foot cedar privacy fence in Maplewood"). Local homeowners browse Facebook heavily, and local groups are full of "anyone know a good fence company?" posts — be findable when they ask.
- **Instagram or a Google Business Profile photo stream:** purely visual, low effort if you're already taking the photos.

One or two posts a week of real work beats daily generic content. Consistency and real photos matter; polish doesn't.

## Paid Advertising: When and Where

Once the free channels are working, paid ads can add volume:

- **Google Local Service Ads and search ads** on terms like "fence installation [town]" capture people actively shopping. Watch your cost per booked job, not per click.
- **Facebook ads** work for fencing because you can show beautiful photos to homeowners in specific neighborhoods — good for filling the schedule in slower months.
- **Timing matters.** Fence demand peaks in spring and early summer. Ramp up marketing in late winter, before the rush, so your pipeline is full when everyone else starts advertising.

Whatever you spend, track where each booked job came from. Many owners discover one channel outperforms everything else and shift budget accordingly.

## The Follow-Up That Closes Quotes

Fence quotes are rarely same-day decisions. The homeowner is collecting bids and thinking it over — which means follow-up wins jobs:

- Send the written quote fast, ideally within 24 hours of the site visit.
- Follow up in two or three days: "Any questions about the estimate?" Then once more a week later.
- When a prospect goes quiet, one final friendly note ("Happy to hold this pricing through the end of the month") revives a surprising number of stalled quotes.

Many fence companies find that simply following up — which most competitors never do — closes a meaningful share of the quotes that would otherwise die quietly.

## Quick Checklist

- [ ] Photographing every completed job from multiple angles
- [ ] Google Business Profile complete, with new photos added weekly
- [ ] Review requested (with a texted link) at every job completion
- [ ] Website has a gallery, material pages, and honest pricing guidance
- [ ] Permit, utility-locate, and property-line questions answered on the site
- [ ] Yard signs placed (with permission) at completed jobs
- [ ] Neighbors of active jobs contacted or door-hangered
- [ ] Referral partners lined up: landscapers, pool builders, realtors
- [ ] Weekly photo posts to Facebook and/or Instagram
- [ ] Quotes delivered within 24 hours with two scheduled follow-ups
- [ ] Lead sources tracked so you know what actually produces jobs
- [ ] Marketing ramped up in late winter ahead of the spring rush
