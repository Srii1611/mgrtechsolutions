More of your potential customers are skipping the ten blue links and asking AI directly: "Who's a good electrician in Springfield?" or "How much does drain cleaning cost near me?" Tools like Perplexity, ChatGPT, and Google's AI answers don't just list websites — they pick a few sources to cite and businesses to mention. That raises a new question for local owners: how do you become one of the sources these AI tools trust and recommend? Here's what's actually known, what's speculation, and what to do about it.

## How AI Answer Engines Decide What to Cite

Traditional Google search ranks pages. AI answer engines do something different: they read pages, pull out facts, and assemble an answer — citing the sources they drew from. To be cited, your business needs to clear two bars:

1. **Be findable.** AI tools lean heavily on the same indexes and rankings as traditional search. If Google and Bing can't find or understand your site, AI tools mostly can't either.
2. **Be quotable.** AI prefers sources that state things clearly and directly — a page that plainly answers a question beats a page that dances around it in marketing language.

There's a third factor unique to local recommendations: **consensus across the web.** When someone asks for "the best plumber in town," the AI looks for agreement — reviews, directory listings, mentions in local articles — not just what your own website says about you.

## The Good News: This Is Mostly Local SEO You Should Already Be Doing

If you've invested in the fundamentals, you're most of the way there. AI visibility currently rides on top of:

- **A strong Google Business Profile.** Complete categories, services, photos, and a steady stream of reviews. AI tools answering local questions draw heavily from this ecosystem.
- **Consistent directory listings.** Your name, address, and phone number matching exactly across Yelp, Angi, the BBB, your local chamber, and industry directories. Inconsistency confuses both search engines and AI.
- **Reviews with substance.** Detailed reviews mentioning specific services ("replaced our 40-gallon water heater same day") give AI quotable evidence of what you do and how well you do it.
- **A website that clearly states the basics.** What you do, where you do it, and how to contact you — in plain text, not buried in images.

## What Makes Content "Citable" by AI

Now for the part that's more specific to answer engines. When Perplexity or a similar tool answers "how much does it cost to repipe a house," it looks for pages where that question is answered directly. To be that page:

**Answer specific questions in plain language.** Structure content around real questions customers ask, and answer them in the first sentence or two — then add detail. Compare:

- Vague: "Repiping costs depend on many factors. Contact us for a quote."
- Citable: "Repiping a typical three-bedroom home usually runs between $4,000 and $10,000, depending on the pipe material, accessibility, and local labor rates."

The second version gives an AI something it can actually quote — with your site as the source. Be honest about ranges and what drives them; hedged non-answers get skipped.

**Use clear headings that match how people ask.** A heading like "How Much Does Repiping Cost?" followed by a direct answer is far easier for an AI to use than a clever heading followed by three paragraphs of buildup.

**Add local specificity.** "In Ohio, most water heater installations require a permit" is the kind of locally grounded fact that national content farms don't have. Local detail is your competitive moat — AI tools need sources that actually know your area.

**Keep facts current.** Pricing guidance from 2019 gets ignored. Review your key informational pages yearly and refresh numbers, regulations, and recommendations.

## Structured Data: The Boring Advantage

Behind the scenes, your website can include "schema markup" — invisible labels that tell machines exactly what's what: this is our business name, this is our service area, these are our reviews, this is our phone number.

For local businesses, the most useful types are LocalBusiness schema (your core details) and FAQ or HowTo schema on informational pages. You don't need to write this yourself — most modern website platforms and SEO plugins generate the basics, and a developer can add the rest in an hour. It won't transform your visibility overnight, but it removes ambiguity for every machine that reads your site, AI included.

## Get Mentioned Beyond Your Own Website

AI recommendations weigh what the wider web says about you. Ways to build that footprint:

- **Local news and blogs.** A quote in a local news story about storm prep, or a guest article in a community publication, creates the kind of third-party mention AI tools notice. Local reporters constantly need expert quotes — make yourself available.
- **Community participation that leaves a trace.** Sponsoring a Little League team or speaking at a home show often generates online mentions and links from local organizations.
- **Industry directories and associations.** Trade association profiles and well-maintained directory listings reinforce who and where you are.
- **Answer questions publicly.** Thoughtful answers in local Facebook groups, Reddit communities, or Nextdoor (following each platform's self-promotion rules) build the kind of reputation trail that occasionally surfaces in AI answers.

## How to Check Whether AI Tools Know You Exist

Spend twenty minutes playing customer:

1. Ask Perplexity and ChatGPT: "Who are well-reviewed [your trade] companies in [your town]?" See if you're mentioned — and who is.
2. Ask the questions your customers ask: "How much does [service] cost in [area]?" Check whose content gets cited.
3. Ask about your business by name. Does the AI describe you accurately? Wrong hours or services usually trace back to outdated listings or an incomplete profile somewhere.

Repeat this quarterly. The businesses that get cited will shift as these tools evolve, and you'll want to watch whether you're gaining or losing ground.

## Keep Your Expectations Grounded

A few honest caveats:

- **This channel is young.** Nobody can guarantee AI citations, and the rules are changing monthly. Anyone selling you guaranteed AI placement is overpromising.
- **It complements local SEO, not replaces it.** The same work — reviews, clear content, consistent listings — drives both. You're not choosing between them.
- **Referral volume is still modest for most local businesses.** Treat AI visibility as an emerging bonus channel layered on top of the marketing that already rings your phone, not as a replacement for it.

## Quick Checklist

- [ ] My Google Business Profile is complete, and my name/address/phone match across all directories
- [ ] My website plainly states what I do, where, and how to contact me — in text
- [ ] I have pages that directly answer real customer questions, with the answer in the first sentence or two
- [ ] Key informational pages include honest local specifics (prices, permits, regulations)
- [ ] Basic schema markup is in place (LocalBusiness, FAQ where relevant)
- [ ] I'm collecting detailed reviews that mention specific services
- [ ] I've pursued at least one third-party mention (local news, association, community site)
- [ ] I've tested what Perplexity and ChatGPT say about my business and my trade locally — and set a quarterly reminder to recheck
