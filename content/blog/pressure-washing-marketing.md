Pressure washing has a marketing superpower most trades would kill for: the results are instantly visible. A driveway goes from gray-green to bright in an afternoon, and everyone who walks past sees it. Yet many pressure washing businesses still live job to job, waiting for the phone to ring. The difference between struggling and booked-out usually isn't equipment — it's a handful of repeatable marketing habits. Here's the full playbook.

## Before-and-Afters Are Your Entire Brand

In pressure washing, visual proof isn't part of your marketing — it *is* your marketing. Build the habit into every single job:

- **Shoot before, during, and after.** The "during" shot — a half-cleaned driveway with a stark dividing line — is oddly satisfying and consistently outperforms polished final photos on social media.
- **Keep the angle consistent.** Same viewpoint before and after makes the transformation undeniable.
- **Short videos beat photos.** A 20-second phone clip of a surface cleaner stripping grime off a sidewalk gets shared. Time-lapses of a full driveway are gold.
- **Collect permission to post.** Most customers are delighted to have their home featured; a quick ask avoids awkwardness later.

Then put that material everywhere: your website gallery, Google Business Profile, Facebook, Instagram, Nextdoor, and local community groups. One good job can generate a week of content.

## Local Search: Where the Booking Intent Lives

When someone searches "pressure washing near me" or "driveway cleaning [town]," they're usually ready to book within days. To catch them:

**Google Business Profile, done right:**

- Correct category (Pressure Washing Service), full services list (house washing, driveway, deck, roof soft wash, commercial), and accurate service area
- Fresh photos added constantly — you have the easiest content pipeline of any trade, so use it
- Reviews requested on every job, at the moment the customer first sees the clean surface. Text the direct review link before you pack up the hoses

**A website that makes booking effortless:**

- Photos front and center, with your best transformations above the fold
- Clear service list and honest pricing guidance — even "driveways from $X" filters out tire-kickers and builds trust
- A tap-to-call number at the top and a quote form that asks for name, phone, address, and what needs cleaning — nothing more
- Before-and-after galleries organized by service type

## The Neighbor Effect: Turn Every Job Into Five

Pressure washing is performed outdoors, in public, often over several noisy hours. Neighbors notice. Systematize that:

- **Yard sign on every job.** Cheap, and it answers the question every passerby is asking: "Who's doing that?"
- **The five-door knock.** Before or after each job, hang a simple flyer or door hanger on the five nearest homes: "We just cleaned your neighbor's driveway at #42 — here's what it looked like." Include the before-and-after. This is arguably the highest-return tactic in the entire trade.
- **Ask the customer directly.** "Who on your street has the greenest siding?" is a playful line that regularly produces a name and a warm introduction.
- **Nextdoor and local Facebook groups.** Neighbors asking "anyone know a good pressure washer?" appear constantly. Having past customers who recommend you by name in those threads is worth more than any ad.

## Repeat Business: The Maintenance Mindset

Surfaces get dirty again — which means your best future customers are your past ones. Most pressure washing businesses never follow up; be the exception:

- **The annual reminder.** Twelve months after a house wash, a short text or email: "It's been about a year since we cleaned your siding — want back on the schedule before pollen season?" Many customers simply forgot it was time.
- **Seasonal triggers.** Pre-sale home spruce-ups (spring), post-pollen cleanups, pre-holiday entertaining (fall), and fleet/storefront refreshes for commercial clients each have natural timing.
- **Maintenance plans for commercial work.** Restaurants, storefronts, HOAs, and property managers need regular cleaning. A standing quarterly agreement transforms unpredictable revenue into a schedule you can plan around.
- **A simple list.** Keep every past customer's name, service, and date in a spreadsheet or basic CRM. The follow-up system only works if the list exists.

## Pricing and Packaging That Lifts Every Booking

How you present prices affects how many quotes convert:

- **Give instant ballparks.** Many competitors play coy on price. Publishing starting prices or offering a fast photo-based quote ("text us a picture of the driveway") wins the customers who just want a straight answer.
- **Bundle surfaces.** "Driveway + walkway + front porch" packages raise the average ticket and give the customer a visible win across their whole property.
- **Quote on-site when it matters.** For house washes and larger jobs, a quick in-person look closes at a much higher rate than a phone estimate — and lets you spot upsells honestly ("your gutters are streaking too; here's what that would add").
- **Follow up on every quote.** A two-line check-in three days later recovers jobs that would otherwise quietly die. Most of your competitors never do this.

## Smart, Modest Advertising (After the Free Stuff Works)

Once reviews, photos, and follow-ups are in place, paid channels can pour fuel on:

- **Google Local Services Ads or search ads** on high-intent terms ("house washing near me") — start small, track cost per booked job, and only scale what pays
- **Facebook/Instagram ads** showing your best before-and-after video, targeted to homeowners within your service area — this trade's visuals are made for these platforms
- **Seasonal postcards** to neighborhoods you want to dominate, timed just before spring and fall peaks

Skip billboard-style brand advertising and any platform where you can't track whether a booking resulted.

## Don't Overlook Commercial and Partnership Work

Residential driveways are the obvious market, but some of the steadiest pressure washing revenue comes from relationships rather than one-off bookings. Restaurants need sidewalks and dumpster pads cleaned on a schedule. Storefronts, gas stations, and parking garages need regular attention. HOAs and property managers control dozens of buildings at once — land one and you've replaced twenty individual sales. Introduce yourself with a simple one-page sheet showing your work, your insurance, and a sample maintenance schedule with pricing. Even a polite "no" puts you on the list for when their current provider drops the ball.
