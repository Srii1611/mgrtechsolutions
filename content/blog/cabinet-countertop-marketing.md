Cabinet and countertop work is a visual, high-ticket, research-heavy sale. Homeowners spend weeks comparing photos, materials, and reviews before they ever contact a shop — which means your marketing has to win two battles: showing up when locals search, and looking like the obvious choice once they find you. This guide covers both, from local rankings to showcasing your work to converting remodel leads.

## Know How Your Customers Actually Shop

Before tactics, understand the buyer. A typical remodel customer:

1. Browses inspiration (Pinterest, Instagram, Houzz) for weeks or months
2. Searches locally: "quartz countertops near me," "custom cabinets [city]," "kitchen remodel cost"
3. Builds a shortlist of 3–5 companies based on reviews, photos, and website quality
4. Requests quotes from 2–3 of them
5. Decides on a mix of trust, communication, and price — rarely price alone

Your marketing needs to be present at every step. Miss step 2 and you never make the shortlist; look weak at step 3 and you're filtered out before the phone rings.

## Own Your Local Rankings First

For a local fabricator or cabinet shop, the Google Map Pack — the three listings with the map at the top of local searches — is the most valuable digital real estate you can win. Many shops find the majority of their inbound leads trace back to it.

### Tune your Google Business Profile

This free listing is the engine of local visibility. Do it thoroughly:

- **Categories:** Choose precisely — "Cabinet maker," "Countertop contractor," "Kitchen remodeler." Your primary category carries the most weight.
- **Service area:** List every town and suburb you actually serve.
- **Photos:** Upload 20+ real photos — finished installs, your shop, your team, slab inventory. Profiles with fresh photos get substantially more engagement.
- **Description:** Write naturally, naming your materials (quartz, granite, quartzite, butcher block) and services (fabrication, installation, custom cabinetry, refacing).
- **Q&A section:** Seed it yourself with the questions you hear weekly: "Do you remove old countertops?" "How long from template to install?" "Do you offer free estimates?"

### Build a review machine

Reviews are both a ranking factor and the first thing prospects read. The shops that dominate local search aren't lucky — they ask.

- Ask every happy customer, every time, at the moment of highest satisfaction: right after install, while they're admiring the finished kitchen.
- Text a direct link to your Google review form — texted links get far more responses than verbal requests.
- Respond to every review, good and bad, within a few days. A thoughtful response to the occasional negative review builds more trust than a perfect wall of five stars.

### Keep your listings consistent

Your business name, address, and phone number should match exactly across Google, Yelp, Houzz, Angi, Facebook, and the BBB. Inconsistencies confuse search engines and dilute local rankings. Audit your listings once a year.

## Your Website: The Showroom That Never Closes

Homeowners judge your craftsmanship by your website before they ever see your work. A dated, slow, photo-poor site quietly tells visitors your work might be the same.

### The pages that win remodel leads

- **A strong portfolio page** — your single most important page (details below)
- **One page per service** — custom cabinets, refacing, countertop fabrication, installation. Separate pages rank better and answer buyers' specific questions.
- **One page per material** — quartz, granite, marble, quartzite, laminate. Comparison searches ("quartz vs granite") are exactly how early-stage buyers research.
- **A service-area page** naming the towns you cover
- **A short estimate-request form** — name, contact info, project type, timeline, plus your phone number. Long forms kill leads.

### Website basics that quietly cost you jobs

- **Speed:** Pages should load in a couple of seconds on a phone. Compress photos before uploading — enormous images are the #1 cause of slow contractor sites.
- **Mobile:** Most visitors are on phones. Test every page on your own device.
- **HTTPS:** Browsers warn visitors away from non-secure sites, especially on pages with forms.
- **Click-to-call phone number** at the top of every page.

## Showcasing Work: Your Portfolio Is Your Salesperson

For a visual trade, photography *is* marketing. Good news: you don't need a professional photographer for every job.

### Build a repeatable photo habit

- **Capture before, during, and after.** Before/after pairs are your most persuasive content.
- **Shoot the details.** Dovetail joints, mitered edges, seam quality, vein matching, hardware close-ups. Details signal craftsmanship to people who know — and impress those who don't.
- **Use natural light.** Shoot in daytime, wipe surfaces, clear the counters. Two minutes of staging transforms a photo.
- **Get a wide shot and three close-ups per job** — a portfolio entry, an Instagram post, and a before/after in five minutes.
- **Ask permission** at the contract stage. Most homeowners are flattered.

### Where to use those photos

- Your website gallery, organized by project type or material
- Google Business Profile (add new photos monthly — freshness helps)
- Instagram and Facebook, where kitchen transformations perform exceptionally well
- Proposals and estimate packets — show similar past projects to the one you're bidding
- A physical album or tablet in your showroom

### Video: the unfair advantage

Almost no local shops do video — exactly why it works. A 30-second walkthrough of a finished kitchen, an install time-lapse, or a clip of your CNC or edge polisher running gives prospects a feel for your operation that photos can't. Post them to your Google profile, social channels, and website.

## Winning the Remodel Lead (and Not Losing It)

Generating the inquiry is half the game; high-ticket remodels are won or lost in the follow-up.

**Respond within the hour when possible.** Remodel customers contact multiple shops, and many hires go to whoever responds first with something useful. Even a quick "Got your request — I'll have questions and a ballpark to you by tomorrow" beats silence.

**Qualify gently, on purpose.** Ask about timeline, budget range, and scope early. You'll spend your estimating hours on real projects instead of tire-kickers — and customers appreciate a shop that takes their project seriously.

**Make quoting visual.** Include photos of two or three similar completed projects with every estimate. It reframes the conversation from price to outcome.

**Follow up at 3 days and 10 days.** Most shops send one quote and vanish. A polite check-in — "Any questions on the estimate?" — closes deals your competitors gave up on.

**Nurture the "not yet" crowd.** Kitchen remodels have long decision cycles. A simple monthly email with a recent project, a material tip, and a seasonal offer keeps you top of mind until they're ready. Past leads and past customers are your cheapest source of future work — and referrals.

## Referral Partners: The Quiet Lead Goldmine

Some of your best leads come from businesses that touch the kitchen before you do:

- General contractors and kitchen/bath remodelers who don't fabricate in-house
- Kitchen designers and interior designers
- Real estate agents preparing homes for sale
- Appliance and flooring showrooms
- Home builders for spec and semi-custom work

Take them to lunch, make your trade pricing clear, be fast and reliable on their jobs, and send referrals back when you can. Two or three strong partnerships can feed a shop more reliably than any ad campaign.

## Paid Ads: Where They Fit

Organic and referral channels are your foundation, but paid can accelerate:

- **Google Local Service Ads / search ads** on high-intent terms ("countertop installation [city]") — pay for ready-to-buy searches, not vague ones.
- **Facebook/Instagram ads** showing your best before/after photos to homeowners in your service area — transformation content is made for these platforms.
- **Retargeting** past site visitors — remodel buyers take weeks; staying visible during their research pays off.

Start small, track which ads produce actual estimates (not just clicks), and scale only what converts. And never run paid traffic to a weak website — fix the showroom first.

## Quick Checklist

- [ ] Fully complete your Google Business Profile with 20+ real photos and accurate categories
- [ ] Set up a text-based review request sent after every install
- [ ] Audit your name/address/phone consistency across all listings
- [ ] Refresh your portfolio page with before/after photos and detail shots
- [ ] Create one website page per service and per material
- [ ] Start a per-job photo habit: wide shot, three close-ups, one before/after pair
- [ ] Add one simple video (walkthrough or install time-lapse) this month
- [ ] Respond to every new inquiry within one business hour
- [ ] Add 3-day and 10-day follow-ups to your estimating process
- [ ] Identify three referral partners and schedule a coffee with each
- [ ] Send a monthly email to past leads and customers featuring one recent project

Cabinet and countertop marketing isn't about tricks — it's about being findable locally, looking unmistakably skilled, and responding like you want the work. Do those three consistently for six months and you'll feel it in your estimate calendar.
