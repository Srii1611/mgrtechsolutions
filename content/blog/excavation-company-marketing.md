Excavation is a different animal than most trades. Your jobs are big, your customers range from homeowners to builders, and one good commercial relationship can be worth a hundred residential calls. That means the marketing playbook that works for a plumber only halfway works for you. Here's a guide built around how excavation work actually gets won.

## Know Who You're Actually Marketing To

Excavation companies typically serve three very different customer types, and each one finds contractors differently:

- **Homeowners** need septic systems, driveways, grading, drainage fixes, and pool digs. They search online, check reviews, and usually get two or three quotes.
- **Builders and general contractors** need site work, foundations, and land clearing on a repeating basis. They hire based on reliability, price, and relationships — and one good GC can keep you busy for years.
- **Property owners, developers, and municipalities** need larger commercial and public work. These jobs come through bids, prequalification, and reputation in the local industry.

Most excavation companies underinvest in the first group and take the second two for granted. The strongest marketing covers all three — but with different tactics for each.

## Winning Homeowner Jobs: Local Search and Trust

For residential work, the game is the same as any local service business: show up when someone searches, look trustworthy, and answer the phone.

### Your Google Business Profile

This is your most important free asset. Set it up completely:

- Choose accurate categories ("Excavating contractor" plus relevant secondary ones).
- List every service you offer — septic installation, land clearing, grading, demolition, drainage.
- Upload photos constantly. Excavation has a huge advantage here: before-and-after shots of a cleared lot or a finished pad are dramatic and convincing. Big machines also just look impressive.
- Collect reviews after every residential job. A homeowner spending $15,000 on a septic system reads every review you have.

### A website that answers real questions

Homeowners hiring an excavation company are usually nervous — it's a big, disruptive, expensive job they've never done before. Your website should calm those nerves:

- A page for each major service, explaining the process in plain English
- Rough guidance on what affects pricing (soil, access, permits, haul-off)
- Photos of completed jobs and your equipment
- Your license and insurance info, front and center
- A phone number that's answered, or a form that gets a fast callback

Answering questions like "How long does a septic installation take?" or "Do I need a permit to clear my lot?" on your site brings in search traffic and pre-sells nervous homeowners before they ever call.

## Winning Builder and GC Work: Relationships Plus Visibility

Contractors don't pick excavation subs from Google. They pick companies they've worked with, companies other trades recommend, and companies they've seen doing good work around town. That doesn't mean marketing doesn't matter — it means it works differently.

**What actually moves the needle:**

- **Be easy to work with, visibly.** Show up when you say you will, communicate schedule changes early, leave sites clean. This is marketing — GCs talk to each other constantly.
- **Stay in touch.** A short check-in call or email to past GC clients every month or two keeps you top of mind when the next project lands. Most excavation companies never do this, so the ones who do stand out.
- **Know your numbers and bid fast.** Many companies find that a clear, professional, quickly delivered quote wins work even when it isn't the cheapest. Slow, sloppy bids signal slow, sloppy work.
- **Be findable when they check you out.** Even a GC who's heard your name will look you up before calling. A decent website, real photos, and visible insurance info close the loop that word-of-mouth opens.

## Winning Bigger Commercial and Public Work

If you want to move upmarket, visibility inside the industry matters more than advertising:

- Get listed on the plan rooms and bid platforms used in your region, and make sure your prequalification paperwork (insurance, bonding capacity, safety record, equipment list) is current and polished.
- Keep a simple capabilities statement — one page on who you are, what you do, what you own, and what you've built — ready to send on request.
- Join local builder and contractor associations. The projects discussed at those meetings rarely hit public bid boards.
- Document big jobs with photos and short write-ups. A portfolio page of completed commercial projects builds credibility with developers faster than any ad.

## The Marketing Assets Most Excavation Companies Ignore

### Your iron is a billboard

Every machine, dump truck, and trailer should carry your company name and phone number in lettering you can read from the road. Job sites sit in public view for weeks — that's free advertising to every neighbor, builder, and developer who drives past. Add a professional yard sign at active residential jobs with permission from the homeowner.

### Neighbors of current jobs

When you're digging up one property, the neighbors are watching — and often thinking about their own drainage, driveway, or lot. A simple door hanger or friendly introduction to adjacent homeowners turns one job into two more often than you'd expect.

### Your reputation for the unglamorous stuff

In excavation, the details people remember are restoration and cleanup: Did you leave ruts in the yard? Did you protect the driveway? Did the site drain properly after you left? Companies that finish clean get referred; companies that don't get complained about in neighborhood Facebook groups — which is also marketing, just the bad kind.

## A Realistic Budget and Where to Put It

You don't need a big budget. For most excavation companies, a sensible priority order looks like this:

1. **Free and nearly free first:** Google Business Profile, reviews, truck lettering, job site signs, staying in touch with past GC clients.
2. **A solid website:** one-time build plus small ongoing updates. This is your credibility hub for every customer type.
3. **Photos, always:** assign someone on the crew to snap before-and-after shots of every job. This feeds your profile, website, and social media for free.
4. **Paid ads last:** if the above is humming, test search ads on high-value services like septic installation. Skip them until your website and reviews are strong enough to convert the clicks.

- [ ] Yard signs placed at active residential jobs
