You do good work. Your customers say so. So why is the schedule full one month and painfully empty the next? For most plumbers, HVAC techs, roofers, and cleaners, the problem isn't the quality of the work — it's that potential customers are slipping through cracks you can't even see. Here are the five most common leak points, and what to do about each one.

## 1. You're Invisible Where People Actually Look

When a homeowner's water heater dies at 7 a.m., they don't flip through a phone book. They pull out their phone and search "water heater repair near me." If your business doesn't show up in the map results or the first few listings, you effectively don't exist for that customer.

The single biggest visibility gap for local service businesses is an incomplete or neglected Google Business Profile. Many owners set it up years ago, never verified it, and haven't touched it since. Meanwhile, a competitor down the road updates theirs weekly — and gets the call.

**What to do:**

- Claim and verify your Google Business Profile if you haven't.
- Fill out every field: services, service area, hours, photos, and a real description of what you do.
- Add fresh photos monthly — job sites, your truck, your team. Real photos outperform stock images.
- Make sure your business name, address, and phone number match exactly everywhere they appear online.

This is free, and it's usually the highest-leverage hour you can spend on marketing.

## 2. Your Website Scares People Off (Or You Barely Have One)

Getting found is only half the battle. When someone clicks through to your website, you have maybe five seconds to convince them you're legitimate and can solve their problem. Common trust-killers include a site that isn't mobile-friendly, no clear phone number, pages that load slowly, and stock photos that scream "template site built in 2011."

A homeowner about to spend $8,000 on a roof replacement is making a judgment call about professionalism based on your site — fair or not.

**What to do:**

- Pull up your site on your own phone right now. Can you read it without zooming? Can you call with one tap?
- Put your phone number at the top of every page, and make it tappable on mobile.
- Replace generic stock photos with real pictures of your crew and completed jobs.
- Add a short, plain-English description of your services and the towns you serve.
- Include at least a few reviews or testimonials on the homepage.

You don't need a fancy site. You need one that loads fast, looks current, and makes it dead simple to contact you.

## 3. You're Too Slow to Respond

Here's an uncomfortable truth most owners don't want to hear: the speed of your response often matters more than the quality of your pitch. Homeowners with a problem typically contact two or three companies, and many businesses find that the first one to respond wins the job a large share of the time — even at a higher price.

If website contact form submissions sit in an email inbox nobody checks until evening, you're handing jobs to competitors. The same goes for missed calls that never get a call back.

**What to do:**

- Route form submissions to a phone number that gets answered, or set up an instant text notification.
- Aim to respond within 5–15 minutes during business hours. An hour is often too late.
- Set up an auto-reply for after-hours inquiries: "Thanks for reaching out — we'll call you first thing in the morning" is far better than silence.
- If you miss a call, call back fast. A callback within 10 minutes still has a real shot; one the next day usually doesn't.

If you're on a roof or under a house all day, this may mean paying for an answering service or having someone in the office own inbound leads. It usually pays for itself.

## 4. You Have No Reviews — Or No Recent Ones

Before calling, most homeowners check reviews. A profile with six reviews from 2021 raises a quiet red flag: is this business still around? Meanwhile, a competitor with 80 reviews and a steady trickle of new ones looks like the safe choice.

The frustrating part is that most satisfied customers would happily leave a review — they're just never asked.

**What to do:**

- Ask every happy customer, every time, right after the job. In person works best.
- Make it easy: text them a direct link to your Google review page while you're still in the driveway.
- Train your techs to ask. A simple "If you were happy with the work, a Google review really helps us out" goes a long way.
- Respond to every review, good and bad. A calm, professional reply to a negative review can actually build trust with people reading it later.
- Never buy fake reviews or offer incentives for positive ones — it violates platform rules and can get your profile penalized.

Reviews compound. Ten new reviews a month for a year transforms how your business looks online.

## 5. Nobody Follows Up After the First Contact

Not every lead is ready to book today. Someone researching a kitchen remodel or a full HVAC replacement might be weeks away from a decision. If your process is "quote it and forget it," you lose every one of those slow-burn leads — and they often end up hiring whoever stayed in touch.

**What to do:**

- Keep a simple list (a spreadsheet works) of every estimate you send, with a follow-up date.
- Follow up 2–3 days after sending a quote: "Any questions about the estimate?" is enough.
- Add a second follow-up a week later. Many jobs close on the second or third touch.
- For seasonal work, reach out proactively — furnace tune-up reminders in the fall, AC checks in the spring. Past customers are your cheapest source of new work.

A basic follow-up habit can recover a meaningful chunk of quotes that would otherwise just die quietly.

## Where to Start

Don't try to fix everything this week. Pick the leak that's costing you the most. For most businesses, that's either the Google Business Profile or response speed — both are fast, cheap fixes with immediate impact. Then work down the list one item at a time over the next month or two.

## Quick Checklist

- [ ] Google Business Profile claimed, verified, and fully filled out
- [ ] Business name, address, and phone number consistent across the web
- [ ] Website readable and clickable on a phone, with a tappable number on every page
- [ ] Real photos of your work on your site and profile
- [ ] Contact form leads trigger an instant notification
- [ ] Inquiries answered within 15 minutes during business hours
- [ ] Every satisfied customer asked for a Google review
- [ ] Direct review link saved and ready to text
- [ ] All reviews responded to, including negative ones
- [ ] Every estimate logged with a follow-up date
- [ ] Two follow-up touches scheduled for open quotes
