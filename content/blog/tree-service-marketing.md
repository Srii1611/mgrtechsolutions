Tree work has a strange demand pattern: panicked calls after storms, a rush of trimming jobs in spring and fall, and dead stretches in between. The tree companies that stay booked aren't luckier — they've built marketing that captures the emergencies, fills the gaps with planned work, and turns one-time storm jobs into repeat customers. Here's the complete playbook.

## Know Your Three Types of Customers

Every marketing decision gets easier once you see who you're marketing to:

1. **Emergency customers** — a tree on the roof, a limb through the fence. They call the first credible company in the map results. Speed and reviews win.
2. **Planned-work customers** — trimming, removals, stump grinding. They research, compare quotes, and take their time. Content, photos, and professionalism win.
3. **Recurring and commercial customers** — property managers, HOAs, municipalities, homeowners with lots of trees. Relationships and reliability win.

A balanced marketing plan speaks to all three.

## Dominate the Local Map Results

For both emergency and planned searches, the Google map pack is where most tree service calls originate.

### Your Google Business Profile

- **Primary category: "Tree service."** Add secondary categories that fit ("Arborist service," "Stump removal service," "Firewood supplier" if applicable).
- **List your full service area** — tree work is worth driving for, so include every town you'll serve.
- **Photos are your superpower.** Few trades produce more dramatic visuals: a crane lifting a massive oak over a house, a perfect crown reduction, a before/after of a cleared lot. Post new photos weekly.
- **Post regular updates** — recent jobs, storm-readiness reminders before forecast weather, seasonal tips.
- **Keep hours accurate**, and if you offer 24/7 emergency response, say so clearly.

### Reviews: The Deciding Factor

When a homeowner compares three tree companies with similar ratings, the one with more recent, more detailed reviews usually gets the call. And in this trade, reviews that mention safety, cleanup, and property protection are worth their weight in gold — that's exactly what nervous homeowners are screening for.

- Text the review link before you leave the property, while the immaculate cleanup is still visible.
- Ask customers to mention what you did ("removed the dead ash over our garage") — detailed reviews sell better than "great job."
- Respond to every review. A professional response to a complaint often impresses readers more than the complaint hurts.

## A Website That Justifies Your Prices

Tree work has a low-barrier competitor problem: every guy with a chainsaw and a pickup is "a tree service." Your website's job is to show why you're worth more than the cheapest quote.

### The Pages You Need

- **A page per service:** tree removal, trimming/pruning, stump grinding, emergency storm response, lot clearing, arborist consultations.
- **A page per priority town** in your service area.
- **Insurance and credentials front and center.** This is your #1 weapon against lowball competitors. Display your liability insurance and workers' comp coverage prominently — then *educate*: a short page or post titled "Why you should never hire an uninsured tree service" converts sticker-shock into relief.
- **A dramatic gallery.** Cranes, rigging, technical removals over structures. Big, real photos.
- **Easy estimate requests:** tap-to-call number everywhere, plus a short form with a photo-upload option ("text us a picture of the tree").

### Content That Brings In Planned Work

Every one of these is a real search in your market:

- How much does tree removal cost? (With honest ranges and what drives price: size, location, access, risk)
- Signs a tree is dangerous and needs to come down
- Do I need a permit to remove a tree in [Town/County]?
- When is the best time of year to trim [common local tree species]?
- Stump grinding vs. stump removal: what's the difference?
- Who's responsible for a neighbor's tree hanging over my property?

One honest, helpful article a month builds a lead-generating library within a year.

## The Storm Playbook: Capture Surge Demand

Storms are your Super Bowl. The companies that prepare capture the surge; the rest watch it go by.

**Before storm season:**

- Publish storm content early: "What to do when a tree falls on your house" and "How to spot storm-damaged limbs before they fall." These rank ahead of need.
- Update your Google profile and website with emergency response details.
- Line up equipment, fuel, and on-call crew arrangements.

**Right after a storm:**

- Post an update to your Google Business Profile immediately: "Crews responding to storm damage across [County] — call for priority scheduling."
- If you run ads, this is when emergency keywords spike — and when a ready campaign pays for itself.
- Photograph (with permission) your storm work — it's your most compelling content.

**A word of ethics:** never price-gouge in a disaster. Storm-chasing with inflated prices burns your reputation in the community you depend on. Fair pricing during a crisis builds a customer base that calls you for the next ten years of planned work.

## Fill the Slow Months Deliberately

Smart tree companies use marketing to shift demand into their dead zones:

- **Winter discounts work.** "Dormant-season pruning" content educates customers that winter is often the *best* time for trimming — then a modest winter special fills your schedule. Many tree companies find off-season discounts preferable to idle crews.
- **Promote stump grinding and lot clearing in the off-season** — ground work doesn't care about foliage.
- **Firewood and mulch.** If you sell byproducts, market them in the off-season for extra revenue and brand visibility.
- **Annual maintenance reminders.** Text or email past customers each fall: "Time to check your trees before winter storms." Your existing customer list is the cheapest marketing channel you own.

## Build the Referral Network

Tree work intersects with nearly every other trade. The referrals flow if you build the relationships:

- **Landscapers and lawn companies** — they don't do big tree work and get asked constantly.
- **Roofers and gutter companies** — they see overhanging limbs and tree damage on every job.
- **Real estate agents** — pre-sale tree issues and post-purchase removals.
- **Insurance agents** — they field "a tree fell on my house" calls before homeowners call anyone else.
- **Electricians and utility-adjacent contractors** — clearance work near lines.

Offer reciprocal referrals and make yourself easy to refer: answer fast, quote professionally, protect the referring party's reputation.

## Your Weekly Routine

- **After every job (5 min):** Photos + review link.
- **Weekly (20 min):** Post photos or an update to Google Business Profile and Facebook.
- **Monthly (1 hr):** Check your map ranking for "tree service near me," add one piece of content, review lead sources.
- **Seasonally:** Storm prep before storm season; winter-promotion push in late fall.
